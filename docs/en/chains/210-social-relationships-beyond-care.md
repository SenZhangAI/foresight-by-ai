# C21 · The migration of social relationships: relationships do not disappear together; time, place, and responsibility are re-bundled

[中文版本](../../zh/chains/210-social-relationships-beyond-care.md)

**Lenses not used**: none — this chain registers a social-landscape entrance only; it does not make “abundance → scarcity” or AI capability order the sole engine. [J-042](../ledger/41-50.md#j-042--shared-experience-and-embodied-presence-remain-the-capacity-ceiling-for-strong-ties-landscape-only) is this chain’s existing landscape-only card, and [J-123](../ledger/123-123.md#j-123--expanding-remote-contact-will-not-automatically-increase-shared-bearing-landscape-only) is its new landscape-only card; neither is upgraded into a formal judgment by this linkage.

**Boundary of this chain:** This is a new social-landscape entrance for courtship, friendship, community, and intergenerational relationships beyond family care. It does not re-register C15’s housing, migration, and fertility judgments, C9’s care judgments, C7’s organisational transmission, or C11’s agency judgments; nor does it upgrade the existing landscape-only J-042, J-047, or J-048 into usable judgments. This chain is currently a **social landscape and research gap**, not a set of judgment cards that has cleared a scale gate.

## 1. Start with relationships, not with AI capability

Relationships have three different carriers that cannot be compressed into “more or less socialising”:

1. **Repeated contact:** stable meetings, calls, shared activities, and mutual help;
2. **Joint bearing:** who absorbs conflict, illness, childcare, unemployment, migration, and other irreversible consequences;
3. **Belonging and recognition:** who counts someone as family, friend, neighbour, colleague, or community member, and who can define the boundary.

Demography, housing costs, migration, work location, care burdens, and institutions first change the cost of contact and the distribution of responsibility; communication tools and AI enter these existing carriers later. This starting point deliberately avoids making “what AI makes abundant” the sole engine, and does not assume that abundance → scarcity must hold.

## 2. Historical calibration: lower contact costs do not automatically enlarge relationship structures

This chain does not yet treat the following material as an established rule. These are calibration candidates for a later pass that must judge each as support, refutation, or undecidable:

- **Candidate supporting cases:** long-distance telephone calls and low-cost air travel lowered the cost of maintaining distance, but did not turn every weak tie into a strong tie; public transport and community facilities expanded the reachable circle, but relationships remained constrained by time, place, and shared responsibility.
- **Candidate counter-cases:** mass social platforms expanded visible contacts and weak ties without automatically increasing durable reciprocity; large-scale migration and urbanisation widened the choice set while potentially weakening everyday neighbourhood and intergenerational contact.

These four groups have not yet been calibrated under a common action definition, denominator, before/after window, and failure-case protocol. “Lower-cost communication mainly expands weak ties,” “long-distance migration re-bundles rather than removes belonging,” and “community facilities can restore relationship density” therefore remain mechanisms to test, not methodological laws.

## 3. The chain: first re-bundle who is present, then who is responsible

Housing and work locations split everyday contact → communication and platforms first restore reachability and weak ties → **contact** can be remote, while joint bearing remains constrained by time, embodied presence, institutional rights, and exitability → family, friendship, community, and intergenerational relationships do not disappear together, but split into “contact remains while responsibility thins” or “responsibility remains while meeting becomes rarer” → organisations, markets, and public institutions re-absorb parts of care, companionship, introductions, conflict mediation, and community functions → the scarce part of relationships may move from “finding someone” to sustained reciprocity, shared presence, and willingness to absorb consequences.

The point is not to announce that one relationship type must decline, but to separate four objects of observation:

- **Courtship and intimate relationships:** matching and expression may become more abundant, while exclusivity, shared finances, care, and conflict repair still require sustained bearing;
- **Friendship:** weak-tie radius may expand without a matching increase in shared experience and fulfilled commitments among strong friends;
- **Community relationships:** a platform can organise an event, but cannot automatically create durable neighbourhood mutual aid, rule enforcement, or conflict repair;
- **Intergenerational relationships:** remote contact and service coordination may increase, while companionship, care authorisation, inheritance, and living arrangements re-bind relationships to households and institutions.

## 4. Future scenes (landscape only, not society-scale forecasts)

- **Near term (2027–2030):** many people may have a larger “reachable network,” while actual shared time remains set by housing, commuting, care, and work arrangements. Family groups, friend groups, and community platforms will take on more reminder and organising work; reducing disconnection is not evidence that relationship quality has improved.
- **Middle term (2030–2035):** as AI drafts invitations, coordinates schedules, translates context, and maintains relationship memory, administrative relationship work may shrink. The hard part may shift toward who attends, who agrees, who returns after conflict, and whether an agent stops representing someone after revocation. This is adjacent to J-047/J-048, but does not copy their cards or treat product usage as evidence of relationship substitution.
- **Farther term:** household, friendship, and community boundaries may be defined more jointly by migration, care services, housing, and institutional rights, rather than by blood or geography alone. This is structural speculation; without long-run, cross-generation, cross-region action data it must not be written as a universal social turn.

## 5. What can be done now

This is not an opportunity claim. Researchers or community organisations can first record a repeatable relationship action rather than collect stories: for example, weekly shared activities among the same households or neighbours, time to restore contact after conflict, whether someone takes over a care or volunteer responsibility after a person exits, and actual shared-presence time for members living apart. Identity of the relationship, responsibility target, observation window, exit, and repair outcome must be recorded together; message counts, friend counts, and platform activity cannot substitute for them.

## 6. Falsification boundary and registered gap

This chain adds [J-123](../ledger/123-123.md#j-123--expanding-remote-contact-will-not-automatically-increase-shared-bearing-landscape-only) as a landscape-only card, so no scene above is treated as a validated forecast. If, across at least three institutional settings and two relationship types (for example friendship and intergenerational ties), long-run comparable data show that shared presence, reciprocal commitment, and conflict repair do not thin as contact becomes remote, and that platform/agent involvement does not change responsibility-bearing, the “contact/responsibility split” should be narrowed or withdrawn. Conversely, if the same relationships show long-run retention, repair, and responsibility fulfilment no worse after agent involvement than after human interaction, the embodied-presence boundary in J-042 and this chain must be re-examined.

**Public gap:** courtship, friendship, community, and intergenerational relationships now have an independently readable entrance, but still lack a common repeated-action definition, deduplicated denominator, historical 2+2 calibration, cross-institution long-run outcomes, and real exit/repair data after human–AI involvement. Preserving this gap is part of the delivery; adjacent organisation, employment, family-care, or AI-authority entrances do not substitute for it.
