# C16 · Culture, Climate, and Everyday Carrying Capacity: six social entrances

[中文版本](../../zh/chains/160-culture-climate-mobility.md)

**Lenses not used**: none — this chain currently has no L1–L9 card carrier; update this line whenever lens attribution changes.

> This chain starts from cultural, institutional, demographic, capital, geographic, and environmental constraints, not from AI. It maps six dimensions together, but only paragraphs backed by cards and checkable indicators are “covered”; the rest are question entrances or landscape only, not definite forecasts.

## Three independent entrances with coverage

### 1. Culture, entertainment, and leisure: the time budget comes before content supply

**Question entrance:** As disposable time, income, and attention habits change, which forms of entertainment move from niche to society scale?

**Covered judgment:** J-112 covers only the time and price constraints on urban middle-income adults’ offline cultural participation, and a testable direction in which short-form video/streaming substitutes for some leisure slots. It does not claim that one platform, genre, or country must win; portions without comparable time-use and payment data remain landscape only.

Venue access, ticket prices, care responsibilities, and working hours → leisure time becomes fragmented → low-friction, pausable media gains a selection advantage → live participation is still produced by social identity and place. Leading indicators are time use, ticket burden, venue access time, and repeat participation.

### 2. Climate and environment: risk travels through insurance, energy, and migration

**Question entrance:** How do extreme weather, energy prices, and adaptation investment change housing, health, food, and migration?

**Covered judgment:** J-113 covers the testable mechanism that households and local governments in high-exposure places first bear environmental shocks through insurance gaps, adaptation investment, and travel disruption. It does not treat a particular disaster as certain or any technology as an automatic solution.

Physical exposure and infrastructure fragility → losses and insurance prices rise → low-income households and fiscally weak places first cut discretionary consumption or move → timely adaptation can buffer losses, while delay converts risk into housing, income, and public-safety pressure. Indicators include premiums, outages/road closures, adaptation spending, food prices, and migration flows.

### 3. Transport and everyday mobility: distance, density, and energy prices

**Question entrance:** How are commuting, care, and shopping trips jointly shaped by transit, road capacity, energy prices, and residential geography?

**Coverage status:** This entrance is included in C16’s boundary and leading indicators, but has no independent judgment card yet. It is therefore landscape only: “travel will automatically switch to one mode” is not covered. The next step is comparable city data on mode, reliability, affordability, and care trips, linked to housing and climate exposure with the same household denominator.
