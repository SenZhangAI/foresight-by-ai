# Near-Term Landscape (about 2026–2029)

> The opening narrative: as generation becomes abundant, bottlenecks move to selection, context, and responsibility.

## Scene: sixty answers before lunch

A team asks for one plan and receives sixty complete versions before lunch: code, interface, tests, and deployment scripts included. The blockage is no longer production. It is deciding which version respects the team's rejected boundaries and who will bear the cost if the chosen version fails.

Lower reasoning cost (J-TECH-001) makes general quality scoring a short window rather than a durable scarcity (J-SCAR-001). Value then moves toward private context (J-SCAR-002) and the irreversibility of real-world action (J-SCAR-003).

## Three migrations

### Production becomes abundant; objective filtering becomes a window

If a quality standard can be formalized, generation systems can absorb the filter. Build workflow-specific tools if useful, but do not treat a general scorer as a permanent moat. See J-SCAR-001.

### Public answers become abundant; private context becomes scarce

The decisive input is accumulated history: what a person or organization tried, rejected, and why. Ownership and the limits of self-description make this more than a chat-memory feature. See J-SCAR-002.

**What can be done now:** turn decision history into an owned, portable, authorizable asset.

### Cheap generation; scarce rollback for execution

Text can be rewritten; payments, deployments, shipments, messages, and contracts carry consequences into reality. Once AI executes, sandboxes, simulation, approvals, and audit rollback become prerequisites. See J-SCAR-003.

**What can be done now:** look for isolation, simulation, approval, and traceability around irreversible actions. Price against expected incident loss, not token savings.

## Boundary

Technology sequencing, organizations, attention and trust, capital, energy, and human needs remain explicit gaps in the ledger. This article is not a claim that the full landscape is complete.

## What could prove this wrong

If a few generic interactions reliably reconstruct individual preferences, private context becomes a short-lived cache. If human-by-human approval covers every irreversible action at negligible cost, rollback infrastructure may not become a category. These are the key counter-signals.
