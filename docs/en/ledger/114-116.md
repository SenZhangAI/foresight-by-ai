# Judgment cards J-114–J-116

[中文版](../../zh/ledger/114-116.md)

This shard registers three non-AI technology-sequencing cards for C12. They do not turn capability arrival into social adoption.

### J-114 · Calibrated single-task embodied systems precede open-world general robots

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-17
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027 to 2035, embodied systems are more likely to stabilize first as calibrated single-task loops in workcells, warehouses, institutions, and controlled services, then expand into multi-task robots for open homes, construction sites, and care; one successful demonstration is not a retained, society-scale service.
- **Audience scale**: Affected population = workers and users in manufacturing, logistics, agriculture, construction, care, and household services, a billion-scale impact reach; actual repeated actor/behaviour denominator = organizations and frontline operators deploying and maintaining the systems, with no common cross-sector denominator yet, so this is not a claim of society-wide adoption; capability type = raises existing professionals' ceiling and organizations, not a claim that people who could not do the work will generally perform open-world tasks.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 **FAIL** — this card judges deployment order and organizational capability, not a hundred-million-person weekly action; it must not be cited as “robots enter all of society.”
- **What can be done now**: An embodied-system deployment lead: within two weeks, take five runs each from one workcell or controlled-institution task and one open-environment task, recording retention, takeover minutes, incidents, downtime, and maintenance renewal; read next (linked to “Leading indicator”): new installs by setting, 12-month retention, human takeover, incident and downtime rate, and maintenance renewal；Use the five runs in each of the two task settings first; industry installs by setting are context only.
- **Lens**: Physical and geographic constraints, supply and demand, capital and institutions, technology regularities; embodiment is one constraint, not a sufficient social outcome.
- **Reasoning chain**: A fixed workcell lets operators calibrate space, objects, tempo, and failure exits → a single-task device can use a safety fence, human takeover, and a maintenance contract to absorb exceptions → an open environment exposes perception, grasping, liability, cleaning, coordination, and safety boundaries at once → more task combinations raise verification and liability costs → repeatable closed loops therefore arrive before general open-world operation.
- **Time window**: 2027-01-01 to 2035-12-31
- **Falsifier**: If, in at least five economies with adequate public statistics, by 2032 open-home or open-construction multi-task embodied systems exceed fixed-workcell or controlled-institution single-task systems in organizations with 12-month retention, weekly repeated use, and continued deployment after an incident, without relying on one-off pilot subsidies, the “controlled single-task first” ordering is falsified; missing comparable retention and repetition denominators means indeterminate.
- **Leading indicator**: New installations by setting, 12-month retention, human-takeover minutes, incident and downtime rates, maintenance-contract renewal, task boundaries, and permission clauses; semiannual / annual.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-073, J-076, J-097
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: General models, cheap actuators, and remote operations could let homes and construction sites share one hardware stack quickly; labor shortages in high-wage settings could subsidize open-world automation first.
- **Against consensus**: Agreement: repetitive, calibratable manufacturing tasks are easier to deploy than open environments. Disagreement: a successful demonstration is not treated as household or care adoption; cross-setting retention data remain thin, so only the ordering is retained.
- **External comparison source**: EXT-45, EXT-44, EXT-47

- **Next review**: 2030-12-31
- **Status**: ACTIVE

### J-115 · Modular generation and short-duration storage precede dispatchable long-duration systems

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-17
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027 to 2035, in new power projects, standardized renewable generation plus short-duration electrochemical storage is more likely to form a repeatable local combination first; long-duration storage, nuclear, or other dispatchable low-carbon systems reaching comparable stable scale still require longer interconnection, permitting, financing, and operating-validation cycles.
- **Audience scale**: Affected population = households, businesses, utilities, developers, and local governments, a billion-scale impact reach; actual repeated actor/behaviour denominator = power-system operators, investors, and permitting institutions, not a personal-consumption action; capability type = raises existing professionals' ceiling; it does not enable people who could not operate a grid to do so.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 does not apply — this card judges infrastructure project portfolios and institutional choice, not repeated personal action.
- **What can be done now**: A power-system planning lead: within two weeks, classify the market’s ten latest additions as renewable plus short-duration storage, long-duration storage, or another dispatchable low-carbon system, recording interconnection, permitting, operation, and cancellation status; read next (linked to “Leading indicator”): interconnection queues, capacity by technology, storage duration, capacity contracts, permit time, and cancellation rate；Use the ten project status records first; total market capacity is context only.
- **Lens**: Supply and demand, physical and geographic constraints, capital and institutions, technology regularities; “clean energy grows” is not independent evidence for this card.
- **Reasoning chain**: Standardized components can be procured, built in parallel, and deployed locally → short-duration storage first settles intraday mismatch and can be settled in existing markets → long-duration and dispatchable systems require longer interconnection queues, permits, fuel or geology validation, and capacity-accounting arrangements → reliability ultimately requires a portfolio of resources → “repeatable modules first, system-level firm capacity later” is more plausible than a single end-state technology arriving at once.
- **Time window**: 2027-01-01 to 2035-12-31
- **Falsifier**: If by 2032, in at least five power markets with public capacity and commissioning data, newly commissioned dispatchable low-carbon or long-duration-storage capacity (actual MW and available MWh) exceeds the standardized-renewable-plus-short-duration combination for three consecutive years, with no longer average application-to-commercial-operation time and without relying mainly on one-off administrative compulsion, this ordering is falsified; if commissioned capacity cannot be separated from queued capacity, indeterminate.
- **Leading indicator**: Interconnection-queue time, commissioned capacity by technology, storage-duration distribution, capacity-market or long-term contracts, permitting time, cancellation rates, and actual availability; quarterly / annual.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-056, J-058, J-063
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Reliability events, carbon constraints, or large public procurement could open a market directly for long-duration and firm capacity; component, land, and transmission constraints could remove the speed advantage of modular options.
- **Against consensus**: Agreement: interconnection, land, permitting, and capacity responsibility constrain energy expansion. Disagreement: falling solar or battery costs are not treated as automatic whole-system substitution; current evidence supports the constraint mechanism more strongly than the exact technology ordering.
- **External comparison source**: EXT-19, EXT-21, EXT-23
- **Source**: [C12 non-AI capability chain · energy systems](../chains/120-technology-capability-sequence.md#7-the-non-ai-capability-chain-from-modular-units-to-the-open-world)

- **Status**: ACTIVE

### J-116 · Chip expansion is first constrained by yield and validated conversion paths, then by nominal capacity

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-17
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027 to 2035, expansion of advanced chips and critical materials is more likely to be constrained first by jointly validated electronic-grade conversion, equipment service, process yield, water, and climate-resilience paths than by the number of announced fabs; only validated substitution paths and stable yield turn nominal capacity into deliverable output.
- **Audience scale**: Affected population = organizations and users across chips, equipment, materials, cloud services, manufacturing, and public infrastructure, a billion-scale impact reach; actual repeated actor/behaviour denominator = specialist organizations conducting process transfer, procurement, validation, and capacity allocation, not a personal action; capability type = raises existing professionals' ceiling.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 does not apply — this card judges supply chains and manufacturing organizations, not repeated personal behavior.
- **What can be done now**: An advanced-manufacturing supply-chain lead: within two weeks, choose three substitute-material or equipment paths and record electronic-grade qualification, yield, conversion time, equipment service, water redundancy, and actual shipments for each; read next (linked to “Leading indicator”): qualification time, yield and shipments, equipment substitutability, utility redundancy, post-event output loss, and insurance terms；Use the three substitute-path validation records first; industry shipment totals are context only.
- **Lens**: Supply and demand, physical and geographic constraints, capital and institutions, technology regularities; “a fab exists” and “deliverable output exists” are kept separate.
- **Reasoning chain**: Critical materials and equipment are often shared conversion nodes or by-products → inventory, a fab, or a second supplier is substitutable only after electronic-grade specifications, recipes, equipment service, and yield are validated → water, climate, and utility interruptions turn nominal capacity into actual loss → the next supply-chain competition therefore centers on validated conversion paths and stable yield, not only announced wafers, megawatts, or buildings.
- **Time window**: 2027-01-01 to 2035-12-31
- **Falsifier**: If by 2032, in at least three advanced-manufacturing ecosystems, newly substituted material or equipment paths achieve at least 90% of the incumbent path’s deliverable output for two consecutive years without public yield, conversion-time, and utility-redundancy validation, and the gap between capacity announcements and shipments is not material, “validated paths precede nominal expansion” is falsified; if firms do not disclose these measures, indeterminate rather than passed.
- **Leading indicator**: Alternative-path certification time, yield and shipments, equipment-service substitutability, water and utility redundancy, actual loss after climate events, long-term procurement, and insurance terms; quarterly / annual.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-069, J-092, J-094, J-095
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Subsidies, vertical integration, and mature-node process transfer could internalize validation costs, making nominal capacity and deliverable output appear nearly simultaneous; a demand shock could make buyers accept lower initial yield.
- **Against consensus**: Agreement: semiconductor supply is jointly shaped by materials, equipment, process, utilities, and geographic concentration. Disagreement: one outage or a risk map is not treated as evidence of expansion order; this is a mechanism ordering, and cross-firm yield and conversion-time data remain necessary.
- **External comparison source**: EXT-69, EXT-70, EXT-74
