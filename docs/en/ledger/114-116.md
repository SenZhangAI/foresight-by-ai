# Judgment cards J-112–J-113

[中文版本](../../zh/ledger/114-116.md)

This shard registers two complete cards in C16, “Culture, Climate, and Everyday Carrying Capacity.”

### J-112 · Leisure culture consumption is constrained by time budgets and access

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-17
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027 to 2032, in high-income cities with comparable data, middle-income adults’ offline cultural participation will remain constrained by fragmented leisure, ticket prices, and access time, while pausable streaming/short-form video substitutes for some everyday entertainment slots; this is directional, not a claim about a platform or national winner.
- **Audience scale**: Affected population = middle-income adults and cultural venues/households in high-income cities, a billion-scale impact reach; actual repeated actors/behaviour denominator = deduplicated adults buying or attending a cultural-entertainment activity at least monthly at T, for which no cross-country common denominator exists, so billion-scale diffusion is not claimed; capability classification = not applicable; it neither raises existing professionals’ ceiling nor lets people who could not do it do it now.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 **FAIL** — this card judges cultural-participation constraints rather than technology diffusion.
- **Lens**: Human needs, supply and demand, social structure, and geography; technology is a medium, not the starting point.- **Reasoning chain**: Work and care fragment leisure → prices, distance, and queues raise fixed costs of offline participation → pausable media lowers coordination costs and occupies small slots → live culture remains supported by identity, sociability, and place → the likely result is substitution and stratification by slot, not one medium replacing culture.
- **Time window**: 2027-01-01 to 2032-12-31
- **Falsifier**: In at least 12 pre-registered high-income cities, if relative to the 2024–2026 baseline the median frequency of offline cultural participation among middle-income adults rises at least 10% for three consecutive years, while time-use data show less fragmentation, ticket burden does not rise, and streaming entertainment time does not increase, the constraint-and-partial-substitution direction is refuted; missing common time-use data is indeterminate.
- **Leading indicator**: Time-use surveys, cultural ticket-price/income ratio, venue access time, monthly repeat participation, and streaming hours; annually.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-109, J-111
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: A four-day week, public childcare, and low-price cultural subsidies could jointly release continuous leisure and lift offline participation; streaming may also stimulate rather than substitute for live attendance.
- **Against consensus**: Agreement: time, price, and access affect cultural participation. Divergence: streaming growth is not treated as inevitable offline decline; data gaps remain landscape only. Retained because time budgets and fixed costs can be tested in city panels.
- **External comparison source**: EXT-120
- **Source**: [C16 section 1](../chains/160-culture-climate-mobility.md)
- **Next review**: 2028-12-31
- **Status**: ACTIVE

### J-113 · Climate shocks first widen inequality through insurance, adaptation, and travel disruption

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-17
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027 to 2035, households and local governments in highly exposed places are more likely to bear environmental shocks first through insurance gaps, inadequate adaptation investment, and travel disruption, with greater risks to discretionary consumption and housing stability among low-income households and fiscally weak places; particular disasters and magnitudes are not certain forecasts.
- **Audience scale**: Affected population = residents, commuters, local governments, and policyholders in highly exposed places, a billion-scale impact reach; actual repeated actors/behaviour denominator = not applicable, because this card judges institutional risk transmission rather than repeated action; capability classification = not applicable; it neither raises existing professionals’ ceiling nor lets people who could not do it do it now.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 **FAIL** — this card judges institutional risk transmission rather than technology diffusion.
- **Lens**: Environmental constraints, supply and demand, capital and institutions, geography, and social structure; it starts from non-AI physical exposure and fiscal capacity.
- **Reasoning chain**: Extreme weather and infrastructure fragility create losses → premiums, deductibles, and interruptions rise → low-income households have less adaptation capital and less ability to move → local budgets allocate among repair, public safety, and long-term adaptation → risk travels through food, housing, income, and migration, widening inequality; timely adaptation can buffer it, so the path is not inevitable.
- **Time window**: 2027-01-01 to 2035-12-31
- **Falsifier**: In at least 20 pre-registered high-exposure places, if for five consecutive years disaster-related premiums/deductibles and road or public-transit disruption days are no higher than the 2024–2026 baseline, low-income housing burden and discretionary-consumption loss do not widen relative to high-income groups, and adaptation spending reaches its registered target, the direction that risk first widens inequality is refuted; missing comparable group or loss data is indeterminate.
- **Leading indicator**: Extreme-weather losses, insurance refusal/premiums, outages and road disruption, local adaptation capital spending, food prices, migration, and housing burden; quarterly/annually.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-061, J-095, J-109
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Insurance pools, central fiscal transfers, and early adaptation investment may convert exposure into affordable costs; high-income households may also bear greater absolute losses through concentrated assets.
- **Against consensus**: Agreement: climate losses, insurance, and adaptation capacity are unevenly distributed. Divergence: no individual disaster or migration outcome is treated as certain; only the observable transmission mechanism is judged, with regional differences open.
- **External comparison source**: EXT-121
- **Source**: [C16 section 2](../chains/160-culture-climate-mobility.md)
- **Next review**: 2029-12-31
- **Status**: ACTIVE
