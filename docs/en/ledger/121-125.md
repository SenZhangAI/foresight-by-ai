# Judgment card J-117

[中文版](../../zh/ledger/121-125.md)

This shard registers the first card for C17, “Religion and Belief.” It separates affiliation, practice, institutional carriers, and normative identity rather than using one “share of believers” as a proxy for the whole social landscape.

### J-117 · Religion differentiates and recombines before it synchronously declines with modernisation

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-19
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027 to 2035, education, urbanisation, and abundant online content will not make religious affiliation and practice decline synchronously worldwide; in multi-country samples with comparable repeated surveys, intergenerational transmission, ritual frequency, migration networks, and community supply are more likely to differentiate and recombine first, while lower formal affiliation will not proportionally eliminate religion’s identity, mutual-aid, and normative functions.
- **Audience scale**: Religious and secular organisations shape identity, rituals, family norms, care mutual aid, and public services for a billion-scale affected population; actual repeated actor/behaviour denominator = people who, under one comparable survey definition, attend worship, pray, take part in a rite, or use religious/secular community mutual aid at least monthly; a cross-country deduplicated denominator is not yet available, so the billion-scale impact reach is not a billion-person weekly-adoption claim; raises existing professionals' ceiling, rather than letting people who could not do it do it now.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 does not apply — this card judges differentiation in institutions, identity, and community functions, not one repeated personal weekly action; the action denominator and cross-country frequency still need to be established.
- **What can be done now**: A social-research or public-service lead: within two weeks, select at least four regions and at least two societies per region with repeated surveys, then code affiliation, monthly practice, intergenerational transmission, ritual participation, community services, and migration background; read next (linked to “Leading indicator”): practice frequency by age cohort, exits and entries, intergenerational transmission, care and mutual-aid coverage by religious/secular organisations, and migration-group differences; first test whether definitions and panels are comparable before comparing totals.
- **Lens**: L1 supply and demand, L4 historical regularity, L5 social regularity, L8 human nature; education and urbanisation change organisational monopoly but do not alone determine the endpoint of belief.
- **Reasoning chain**: Education, urbanisation, and state-service expansion weaken some religious organisations’ monopoly over knowledge and public affairs → fixed rituals and intergenerational transmission decline for some groups → marriage, funerals, mutual aid, moral identity, migration networks, and crisis care still require organisational carriers → religious organisations, secular charities, state services, and digital communities compete over those functions → demography, migration, regulation, and family transmission differentiate outcomes by society and generation → practice, organisational boundaries, and functions therefore reorganise before a synchronous global move to “no belief.”
- **Time window**: 2027-01-01 to 2035-12-31
- **Falsifier**: If by 2035, in at least ten societies across four regions with at least two comparable survey waves, all four measures—affiliation, monthly practice, intergenerational transmission, and community participation—are each at least 20% below their 2025 baseline, while neither religious nor secular organisations retain material functions in marriage/funerals, mutual aid, care, or identity norms, and migration groups and younger cohorts show no offsetting differentiation, the claim that differentiation and functional recombination precede synchronous decline is falsified; non-aligned survey definitions or one-wave evidence is indeterminate.
- **Leading indicator**: Affiliation and monthly practice by age, education, migration background, and place; intergenerational transmission; coverage of marriage/funerals, care, charity, and mutual aid; retention, exit, and entry in religious and secular communities; annual or biennial.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: —
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Education, urbanisation, welfare states, and generational replacement could jointly weaken affiliation, practice, transmission, and organisational services, leaving neither religious nor secular communities able to retain the former functions; digital communities may provide content without stable mutual aid, so “recombination before decline” may overstate institutional carrying capacity.
- **Against consensus**: not completed this round; the independent pass provisionally matches the working premise that modernisation weakens some traditional religious monopolies while secularisation paths differ across societies, but no EXT source has been registered yet. Disagreement: this does not extrapolate the historical path of parts of Europe into a synchronous global endpoint, nor migration or crisis mobilisation into universal revival; the retained claim is the four-measure, falsifiable mechanism that differentiation and functional recombination come first.
- **External comparison source**: This round has not completed an external comparison; comparable surveys and historical-case sources will be registered in the next round.
- **Source**: [C17 Religion and Belief](../chains/170-religion-and-belief.md#3-chain-logic-why-differentiation-and-recombination-come-before-one-global-endpoint)
- **Next review**: 2029-12-31
- **Status**: ACTIVE

### J-118 · Productivity growth first differentiates claims, then determines whether income diffuses

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-19
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027 to 2035, in economies affected by high-productivity technologies, increased productivity or output will not automatically become sustainable income and wealth growth for most households; where housing, capital, and complementary-asset supply is constrained, gains are more likely first to appear in asset returns, rents on scarce positions, and returns to complementary skills, while bottom-household disposable capacity catches up only where bargaining power, public services, taxation, or broad asset ownership expand alongside productivity.
- **Audience scale**: Affected populations whose distribution of income, housing, education, care, and political choice is shaped by distribution are billion-scale; the countable action denominator is households tracked by income decile in comparable national panels for disposable income, net wealth, and housing burden, not a claim that the billion-scale affected population performs one weekly action; raises existing professionals' ceiling rather than making non-specialists broadly perform a new action.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 does not apply—the card judges distributional structure and household outcomes, not a repeated individual action; comparable cross-country household panels remain to be assembled.
- **What can be done now**: Researchers and policy designers can build a like-for-like panel across at least four economies, tracking productivity, labour-income share, bottom real disposable income, household net wealth, housing cost, and access to public services; first watch the divergence between asset returns and wages, bottom-household debt, and rent burden before interpreting average output as diffusion.
- **Lens**: L1 supply and demand, L2 technology regularities, L3 capital and power, L4 historical regularities, and L5 social regularities; “abundance → scarcity” is only a supporting question about whether more production is blocked by claims and access.
- **Reasoning chain**: technology and organization first raise productivity in some tasks → concentration of complementary assets such as housing, land, capital, finance, networks, and permits determines who obtains rents first → labour substitutability, bargaining power, occupational entry, mobility, and transfers determine whether income diffuses → wealth gaps feed back through housing, education, care, and waiting risk into the next generation → taxation, public services, labour institutions, and broad asset ownership can interrupt or amplify that feedback → productivity growth therefore first differentiates claims, while whether it becomes income and wealth growth for most households depends on institutional and supply intermediaries.
- **Time window**: 2027-01-01 to 2035-12-31
- **Falsifier**: If by 2035, in at least four economies spanning different institutions, productivity growth is accompanied by sustained improvement in bottom-40% households’ real disposable income, net wealth after debt, and housing burden, while asset returns and returns to complementary skills do not widen relative to bottom households—or if this improvement occurs mainly in economies without changes in bargaining power, public services, taxation, or broad asset ownership—then the claim that gains first differentiate claims and are then diffused through institutions is falsified; inconsistent definitions, averages without distribution, or a single-year observation are indeterminate.
- **Leading indicator**: Labour-income share, real disposable income, net wealth, and rent/debt burden by income and wealth decile; housing and land prices relative to wages; returns to complementary skills and assets; tax transfers and the in-kind value of public services; annual or quarterly.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: —
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Productivity gains will rapidly lower the cost of housing, education, care, and information and, through new occupations and broad ownership, benefit bottom households together; concentration is only a short transition, so institutions need not change materially for gains to diffuse. If multiple economies with different institutions show that result, this card’s intermediary mechanism should be downgraded.
- **Against consensus**: not completed this round; the independent pass retains the mechanism that technological growth may widen capital returns and skill premia while distribution depends on institutions. It diverges from both “AI will cause universal unemployment” and “growth will necessarily be broadly shared,” requiring joint series for bottom households, assets, and housing; the historical 2+2 is only a candidate set to register and has not completed reproducible calibration.
- **External comparison source**: Not completed this round; the next round should register comparable panels for income deciles, wealth, housing, and tax systems.
- **Source**: [C18: Income, wealth, and inequality](../chains/180-income-wealth-inequality.md#3-the-chain-from-production-to-claims)
- **Next review**: 2030-12-31
- **Status**: ACTIVE
