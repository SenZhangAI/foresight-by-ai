# J-120–J-121 · C20 Boundaries Before Capability

[中文](../../zh/ledger/120-121.md)

### J-120 · Rights around data and model outputs will layer first; liability will remain attached to recoverable organizations

- **ID**: J-120
- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-22
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027–2035, rights around data access, training provenance, model-output licensing, and agent-incident liability are more likely to become layered, revocable, and scenario-specific than to become one universal “data ownership” or an independent AI liability subject; compensation and correction in consequential settings will remain primarily attached to recoverable organizations, insurance, and contractual networks.
- **Audience scale**: Affected population = data subjects, consumers, and people touched by automated decisions, a billion-scale impact reach (not evidence of billion-scale repeated action); actual repeated actors/behaviour denominator = organizational staff who configure data permissions, model-output licences, or agent-liability procedures at least weekly; no cross-system deduplicated denominator exists, `UNKNOWN`; capability type = raises existing professionals' ceiling, not enabling people who previously could not perform a new liability-handling action.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: The affected population is a billion-scale impact surface; actual repeated-actor denominator is `UNKNOWN`, so this does not claim social-scale diffusion; raises the ceiling of existing professionals; Gate 1 fails.
- **Lens**: L1 supply and demand, L4 historical regularities, L5 social regularities, L7 institutions and power, and L8 human nature; “abundance → scarcity” is only used to test whether replicable outputs make responsibility and recoverability new constraints.
- **Reasoning chain**: Data and model outputs are replicable but provenance, privacy, training contribution, and real-world consequences are not fully replicable → access, use, returns, revocation, and proof split into different rights → consequential settings need recoverable organizations, insurance, audits, and human review → cross-system agent incidents expose jurisdiction and compensation conflicts → institutions extend layered permission and liability carriers rather than granting models independent legal personality.
- **Time window**: 2027-01-01 to 2035-12-31
- **Falsifier**: By 2035-12-31, in at least three materially different legal systems, most high-consequence model incidents are directly adjudicated through a uniform model-provider/model-subject liability rule and compensation and correction no longer rely mainly on deploying organizations, contracts, insurance, or human liability carriers; if this is not caused by sampling or reporting failure, the judgment is falsified.
- **Leading indicator**: Every six months, in at least three pre-registered materially different jurisdictions, register the population of high-consequence model incidents (domestic and cross-border deployments; the same task type, time window, and public-reporting coverage denominator within each jurisdiction). For each incident record whether a uniform model-provider/model-subject rule directly adjudicated liability and whether compensation and correction still relied on deploying organizations, contracts, insurance, or human carriers, together with missing and indeterminate cases. Compute `R = incidents directly adjudicated by the uniform model rule without those carriers / all determinate incidents` only when public-reporting coverage reaches 80% and indeterminate cases are no more than 20% in each jurisdiction; `R > 0.5` in all three jurisdictions for two consecutive years, with coverage not falling, is a counter-signal.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-055, J-098
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: A uniform model-provider liability rule, compulsory insurance, or international mutual-recognition regime could appear quickly, concentrating liability in model providers or a new subject rather than in a deployment-organization network.
- **Against consensus**: External comparison was not completed this round; the independent reasoning agrees with the general governance consensus that consequential AI needs accountability, audits, and human oversight, but does not turn that principle into globally deployed layered rights or treat independent AI personality as an imminent certainty; it retains the cross-system incident-denominator gap.
- **External comparison source**: Not completed; comparable sources for data rights, model licensing, insurance, and agent-incident case law across jurisdictions remain to be registered.
- **External comparison status**: `INCOMPLETE_NOT_ATTEMPTED`; source set: `NONE`. Gap: comparable rules and incident samples across jurisdictions were not registered; next: register rules, cases, and insurance terms in at least three materially different jurisdictions, then update this status.
- **Source**: [C20 Boundaries Before Capability](../chains/200-law-property-liability-and-geopolitics.md#3-chain-logic-expanding-capability-first-creates-boundary-disputes-and-institutions-answer-with-layered-permission-and-liability-carriers)
- **Next review**: 2029-12-31
- **Status**: ACTIVE

### J-121 · Cross-border institutions will zone around critical dependencies; global rules will not converge automatically

- **ID**: J-121
- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-22
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027–2035, states and large organizations are more likely first to rewrite cross-border availability of data, compute, and critical infrastructure through licensing, export controls, procurement, standards, localization, and mutual-recognition agreements, producing institutional zones with different interoperability, rather than automatically converging on one global rulebook; this is not a forecast of war or of one country’s inevitable victory.
- **Audience scale**: Affected population = residents and cross-border service users, a billion-scale impact reach touched by data flows, infrastructure dependence, and institutional-zone differences; actual repeated actors/behaviour denominator = staff and institutions representing states, platforms, or critical-infrastructure organizations that execute cross-border licensing, procurement, standards, or supply switches monthly; no cross-country deduplicated denominator exists, `UNKNOWN`; capability type = raises existing professionals' ceiling, not enabling people who previously could not execute national cross-border coordination.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: The affected population is a billion-scale impact surface; actual repeated-actor denominator is `UNKNOWN`, so this does not claim billion-scale execution; raises the ceiling of existing professionals; Gate 1 fails.
- **Lens**: L1 supply and demand, L4 historical regularities, L5 social regularities, L7 institutions and power, and L8 human nature; it does not extrapolate C14’s security-budget and electoral response into war, nuclear deterrence, or global polarisation.
- **Reasoning chain**: Critical infrastructure, data, and compute have geographic dependence and switching costs → states and large organizations gain bargaining chips with enforceable conditions → cross-border conflicts first enter licensing, procurement, standards, export, and localization procedures → mutual-recognition agreements lower some friction while preserving institutional differences → firms and residents face zoned availability and switching costs rather than one automatically unified global rulebook.
- **Time window**: 2027-01-01 to 2035-12-31
- **Falsifier**: Pre-register at least three materially different jurisdictions and one common set of cross-border data, compute, and critical-infrastructure transactions. By 2035-12-31, if each jurisdiction uses one enforceable set of licensing, liability, data-flow, and dispute-adjudication rules for those transactions, and localization, export-control, and mutual-recognition differences leave no more than a 5-percentage-point cross-jurisdiction gap in successful service availability and no more than a 1.2x ratio between median switching costs and the lowest-cost jurisdiction for two consecutive years, the judgment is falsified; absent measurement error, a single agreement or textual similarity is not enough.
- **Leading indicator**: Each year, on the same pre-registered set of at least three materially different jurisdictions and the same set of cross-border data, compute, and critical-infrastructure transactions, compare the enforceable versions of licensing, liability, data-flow, and dispute-adjudication rules; set `S=1` only when the scope, enforcement procedure, and adjudication path of all four rule classes are identical across the three jurisdictions. Also compute the maximum cross-jurisdiction gap in successful service availability `ΔA` and median switching cost relative to the lowest-cost jurisdiction `R_switch`. Report a counter-signal matching the falsifier only when, for two consecutive years, `S=1`, `ΔA ≤ 5` percentage points, `R_switch ≤ 1.2`, and weighted restriction strength falls; any missing condition is not a falsifier signal.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-059, J-060, J-061, J-062
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Multinational firms, mutual-recognition standards, and diversified supply could make critical dependencies substitutable commodities, leaving institutional differences textual rather than affecting real availability; a major crisis could also produce broad rule convergence.
- **Against consensus**: External comparison was not completed this round; the independent reasoning agrees with the general claim that critical-technology supply chains are affected by state policy, but does not extrapolate current export controls or alliance commitments into permanent zoning, and does not equate zoning with war; it tests the claim through enforceable licences, switching costs, and actual service availability.
- **External comparison source**: Not completed; time-series sources for cross-border data, compute, and critical-infrastructure licences and procurement rules remain to be registered.
- **External comparison status**: `INCOMPLETE_NOT_ATTEMPTED`; source set: `NONE`. Gap: a unified series for cross-jurisdiction rule changes, mutual recognition, and real availability was not completed; next: register licensing, procurement, standards, and availability data for at least three institutional zones, then update this status.
- **Source**: [C20 Boundaries Before Capability](../chains/200-law-property-liability-and-geopolitics.md#3-chain-logic-expanding-capability-first-creates-boundary-disputes-and-institutions-answer-with-layered-permission-and-liability-carriers)
- **Next review**: 2029-12-31
- **Status**: ACTIVE
