# Judgment Card J-122

[中文版本](../../zh/ledger/122-122.md)

This shard registers the transport and everyday-mobility card for C16, “Culture, Climate, and Everyday Carrying Capacity.” It does not count existing embodied-intelligence cards as transport coverage, and it does not turn one transport mode into a certain winner.

### J-122 · Everyday mobility stratifies first by reliability and care responsibility, not by automatic switching to one mode

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-22
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027 to 2035, in high-income cities with comparable data, commuting, care, and shopping trips are more likely to stratify by reliability, affordability, residential density, and care responsibility; lower-income households and households making frequent care trips will first absorb waiting, transfer, and disruption costs, rather than everyone automatically switching to one transport mode.
- **Audience scale**: Affected population = urban commuters, carers, households with children or older adults, transit users, and local transport authorities, a **billion-scale** impact reach; repeated-action denominator = unique adults and households making a commute, care, or shopping trip at least weekly, for which no cross-city common denominator exists, so this is not a claim of a billion weekly actions; raises existing professionals' ceiling: neither; lets people who could not do it do it now: neither.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Affected population is a billion-scale impact reach; the repeated-action denominator is not unified, so this is not a claim of a billion weekly personal actions; capability type: neither raises existing professionals' ceiling nor enables people who previously could not do it; Gate 1 **FAIL**.
- **What can be done now**: For a city transport or public-service lead: sample 20 households’ commuting, care, and shopping trips over two weeks, recording waiting, transfers, cost, cancellation reasons, substitute routes, and the person receiving care; watch (the “leading indicators”): reliability, total trip time, cost/income ratio, disruption days, care-trip access, and mode mix by income and care responsibility; start with the 20 household trip records, using city totals only as context.
- **Lens**: L1 (supply and demand), L4 (historical regularity), L5 (social regularity), L6 (the physical-constraint half of the technology regularity), L7 (institutions and power), L8 (risk and accountability in human nature); technology is one transport carrier among others, not the starting point.
- **Reasoning chain**: Residential geography and care responsibilities fix trips that must happen → road capacity, transit frequency, energy prices, and extreme weather jointly change reliability and cost → households with less time slack and greater care responsibility absorb waiting, transfers, and cancellations first → households combine modes and adjust activity radii rather than synchronously switching to one mode when a technology appears → transport inequality first appears as unequal access to reliable arrival.
- **Time window**: 2027-01-01 to 2035-12-31
- **Falsifier**: In at least 12 pre-registered high-income cities, if, relative to the 2024–2026 baseline, reliability, affordability, and care-trip access improve by at least 10% for every income and care-responsibility group for three consecutive years, while differences in mode mix and cancellation/disruption also do not widen, then the direction that reliability and care responsibility produce stratification rather than single-mode substitution is falsified; without a common household denominator or subgroup data, record it as undecidable.
- **Leading indicator**: On-time performance, waiting and transfer time, trip cost/income ratio, road and transit disruption days, care-trip access, mode mix, and cancellation rate by income and care responsibility; quarterly/annual.
- **Confidence**: Low
- **depends-on**: J-096, J-109, J-113
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Denser housing, free or low-cost transit, remote services, and reliable autonomous shuttles could jointly reduce waiting and care-trip costs, narrowing accessibility differences; lower energy prices could also keep private travel affordable.
- **Against consensus**: not completed this round; the independent reasoning does not treat electrification, autonomous driving, or transit expansion as automatic universal mode switching, and retains a narrow judgment about stratification by reliability, affordability, and care responsibility; cross-city household denominators and long-run panels remain open.
- **External comparison source**: not completed this round; no independent external source is registered.
- **External comparison status (2026-10-22)**: `UNVERIFIED`; the independent reasoning is registered, but comparable cross-city material has not yet been completed; this card must not be described as externally validated. Next: establish a common household denominator using the leading indicators, add city panels, and re-evaluate confidence.
