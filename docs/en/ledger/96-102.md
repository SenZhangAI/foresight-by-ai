### J-096 · Ageing and smaller households move part of care toward formal and coordination layers

- **Proposed date**: 2026-09-28
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027–2035, ageing and smaller households will move part of care from invisible family obligations toward formal services, insurance/public payment, and cross-institution coordination; this will not mean that hands-on family care exits.
- **Audience scale**: The affected population is a constructed tens-of-millions impact reach of care recipients, family members, and care workers. The actual repeated actors are adults who provide hands-on care or care coordination at least weekly; available comparable evidence supports only subgroups at no more than the million-scale, not billion-scale diffusion. The change raises existing professionals' ceiling, reallocates existing caregivers’ burden, and lets households that cannot sustain care use formal services.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 (scale) fails: there is no global deduplicated weekly actor denominator, so this remains an institutional/occupational judgment; Gate 2 (substitution) partly passes: formal services replace part of families’ continuous care time, not the total care demand; Gate 3 (carrier) partly passes: insurance, public services, and existing care institutions provide carriers, with uneven coverage and quality; Gate 4 (decision rights) partly passes: payers, institutions, and families must change together, without one uniform authority or local closed loop; Gate 5 (recurring net burden) remains unverified: comparable cross-system series on the long-run affordability and net burden of formal versus family care are missing. Unlocked gates do not support society-scale diffusion; they support an institutional/occupational judgment that raises existing professionals’ ceiling.
- **What can be done now**: Prioritise measurement and narrow services for care coordination, scheduling, exception escalation, authorisation records, and service traceability; do not make global household-robot adoption or hundred-million weekly actors a product premise.
- **Lens**: Demography, supply/demand and payment, institutional substitution, family relations.
- **Reasoning chain**: Persistent elder-care demand → less available family time → scheduling, leave, and continuity gaps in unpaid care → insurance/public services and coordination roles carry some actions → families retain authorisation, companionship, and exception decisions.
- **Time window**: 2027–2035.
- **Falsifier**: By 2033, in at least four comparable demographic and care systems, family-care time, formal-care spending, and coordination roles have not moved toward formal/coordination carriers; or any movement is fully explained by a short-term business cycle rather than demographic structure and household size.
- **Leading indicator**: Daily/weekly frequency of informal care, care leave and labour-force exit, long-term-care insurance/public-care spending, home-care hours, coordination roles, and waiting time; annually.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-066, J-073.
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Productivity and migration expand purchasable care supply, or delayed disability keeps demand below what demographic structure implies, so the division between household and formal carriers barely changes.
- **Against consensus**: **Partly consistent**: OECD comparable surveys show that daily/weekly informal care is real, while Japan and Sweden show that institutions can reorganise the carrier. **Boundary**: these materials do not provide a global caregiver denominator or prove that formal services expand in every system. **Why retain**: family time cannot be stored; institutions can change who acts, but cannot erase continuity of care.
- **External comparison source**: EXT-62, EXT-63, EXT-64.
- **Source**: [C9: Ageing and institutional care](../chains/90-aging-care-and-institutional-substitution.md).

### J-097 · Care assistance stabilises first in institutions and controlled services; open-home general-purpose robots lag

- **Proposed date**: 2026-09-28
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027–2038, contact transfer, exception observation, and standardised care assistance are more likely to form stable routines first in institutions and controlled home-care services; general-purpose robots in open-ended homes will not constitute social-scale diffusion in this window.
- **Audience scale**: Direct actors are no more than a million-scale population of care institutions, care workers, maintenance providers, and payers; the main effect raises existing professionals' ceiling. Care recipients and families are a tens-of-millions impact reach, not the denominator for one high-frequency technical action.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 (scale) fails: institutional procurement and household deployment are low-frequency organisational decisions, and open-ended household tasks have no deduplicated weekly actor denominator, so this remains an occupational/institutional judgment; Gate 2 (substitution) partly passes: single-task devices can replace parts of transfer or observation work, not the care relationship or all exception handling; Gate 3 (carrier) passes in institutions but remains unlocked at home: institutions have procurement, training, maintenance, and records, while heterogeneous homes lack a common carrier; Gate 4 (decision rights) passes locally in institutions but remains unlocked at home: relatives, providers, payers, and liable parties must align; Gate 5 (recurring net burden) remains unverified: comparable series on incidents, maintenance, modification, and human-takeover costs are missing. Unlocked gates do not support society-scale diffusion; the effect raises existing professionals' ceiling rather than letting people who could not do the activity do it now at scale.
- **What can be done now**: Validate single-task equipment in institutions or controlled home-care services using retention, human takeover, incident, and maintenance measures; use remote observation, reminders, scheduling, and exception escalation as household entry points rather than treating a general-purpose home robot as a society-scale market premise.
- **Lens**: Task decomposability, institutional economics, physical and liability constraints, diffusion gates.
- **Reasoning chain**: Repetitive tasks are easier to standardise → institutions concentrate procurement and training → liability, maintenance, and records can bind to an organisation → controlled settings stabilise first → heterogeneous homes, rare exceptions, and externalised liability slow general-purpose devices.
- **Time window**: 2027–2038.
- **Falsifier**: By 2035, across multiple income levels and at least three institutional settings, general-purpose home-care robots are used continuously by large numbers of households, cover at least three cross-space tasks (such as transfers, cleaning, and exception handling), and have retention, incident, and maintenance performance no worse than institutional deployments. Demonstrations, pilots, or single-task devices do not falsify this card.
- **Leading indicator**: Paid institutional deployments and retention, equipment hours per thousand recipients, months of continuous household use, cross-task success, human takeover and incident rates, and maintenance/modification cost; annually.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-073, J-074, J-066, J-069.
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Low-cost hardware/software, home modification, and insurance payment mature together, making homes controlled environments; or labour scarcity forces high subsidies and bypasses natural diffusion speed.
- **Against consensus**: **Partly consistent**: embodied systems’ diffusion in factories and contact-transfer prototypes support “capability can exist while institutions carry it first.” **Boundary**: current sources lack cross-country deployment, retention, and household incident data, so institutional priority is not an observed fact yet. **Why retain**: open homes combine physical uncertainty, externalised liability, and maintenance cost; historical diffusion suggests these can bind before the capability curve.
- **External comparison source**: EXT-43, EXT-44, EXT-45.
- **Source**: [C9: Ageing and institutional care](../chains/90-aging-care-and-institutional-substitution.md).
- **Next review**: 2027-12-31.

### J-098 · Authority before intelligence: consequential settings form revocable tiered agency first

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-01
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027–2035, human–AI relationships in consequential settings are more likely to stabilise first as tiered agency attached to the liability of a person, organisation, or public institution. Authorisation, revocation, logs, and appeals become visible interfaces before AI is broadly recognised as an independent liable subject.
- **Audience scale**: Direct actors are a million-scale-or-smaller population of high-liability professionals, compliance and safety staff, service providers, and public institutions; affected customers, workers, and residents are a tens-of-millions impact reach. It raises existing professionals' ceiling and organisations' execution capacity; it does not give people without liability standing broad access to irreversible decision rights. The necessary frequency boundary remains unverified: no cross-system deduplicated weekly-actor denominator is available, so the million-scale figure is an occupational/institutional upper bound, not a society-scale weekly count.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 fails: authority and liability are low-frequency organisational/occupational actions with no society-scale weekly actor denominator; Gate 2 partly passes: AI can replace records, matching, and execution steps but not authority or liability; Gate 3 passes inside existing organisations and remains unlocked for unsupported personal relationships; Gate 4 partly passes: law and contracts can close local loops, while cross-party revocation and appeals remain non-uniform; Gate 5 remains unverified because cross-industry series for incidents, insurance, training, and audit burden are missing. This is therefore an institutional/occupational judgment, not a society-scale forecast of AI personhood.
- **What can be done now**: Make authority scope, revocation, overreach alerts, action logs, human takeover, appeals, and loss allocation first-class product or procurement fields. Measure them in one narrow workflow with an existing liable party rather than starting from an “AI employee” or “AI friend” personhood narrative.
- **Lens**: Agency institutions, legal liability, organisational coordination, power, and relationship boundaries.
- **Reasoning chain**: Society needs durable delegation → institutions split authority, revocation, oversight, evidence, and liability → existing liable parties carry digital agents first → AI expands execution inside controlled boundaries → the human–AI relationship first becomes a rights-and-liability relationship.
- **Time window**: 2027–2035.
- **Falsifier**: By 2033, in at least three consequential institutional settings, large numbers of continuously used AI agents operate outside the liability of a person, organisation, or public institution without traceable authority, revocation, logs, appeals, and compensation arrangements; or these interfaces become widespread without changing procurement, incident handling, or practical limits on agent authority. One-off demonstrations, no-liability chat, and pilots do not falsify the judgment.
- **Leading indicator**: Share of consequential-AI contracts with authority, revocation, and overreach clauses; sustained deployments with human takeover and appeal paths; liability attribution and insurance exclusions after agent incidents; cross-party revocation completion time; observe quarterly or annually.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-009, J-031, J-055, J-065, J-070.
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Platforms may bundle liability, insurance, and audit for free, preventing a durable market for independent authorisation infrastructure; or some regimes may allow low-risk agents to acquire de facto autonomy first and force legal recognition later.
- **Against consensus**: **Partly consistent**: existing governance frameworks commonly emphasise human oversight, logs, and accountability. **Boundary**: these materials do not prove that tiered agency will diffuse in the same order across industries, or that AI can never receive a limited legal status. **Why retain**: the carrier for agency predates the tool, and liability and revocation are institutional interfaces that fluent language cannot replace.
- **External comparison source**: EXT-10, EXT-14, EXT-15.
- **Source**: [C11: Authority before intelligence](../chains/110-authority-before-intelligence.md).
- **Next review**: 2027-06-30.

### J-099 · Embodied automation re-bundles occupational tasks before it eliminates occupations

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-01
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027–2035, embodied systems are more likely to remove repetitive, hand-off-able task segments in warehousing, manufacturing, agriculture, and care, then re-bundle exception handling, relationship work, and responsibility sign-off; capability curves alone will not eliminate whole occupations at the same time.
- **Audience scale**: The affected population is a constructed tens-of-millions impact reach of workers and service users in these sectors. Actual repeated actors are workers in adoption-dense regions performing the measured task segment weekly; current occupation and deployment data can construct no more than a million-scale denominator, not society-scale diffusion. It raises existing professionals' ceiling; specifically, it raises existing professionals' ceiling for exception and responsibility work while assigning structured actions to systems that previously did not perform them.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 fails: no common deduplicated actor denominator exists across task segments, so this remains occupational/organisational; Gate 2 partly passes: equipment replaces a task, not the whole service; Gate 3 varies by scene; Gate 4 requires local employer, insurer, union, and regulator closure; Gate 5 remains unverified because takeover, downtime, and training burdens lack a time series. Unlocked gates do not support a net-employment or society-scale claim.
- **What can be done now**: Record automation frequency, human takeover time, exception wages, equipment ownership, and downtime risk by task bundle rather than making training or redundancy decisions from “occupation replaced.”
- **Lens**: Task decomposition, historical diffusion, supply/demand, labour institutions, and liability constraints.
- **Reasoning chain**: Structured, hand-off-able tasks clear unit economics first → task bundles split inside occupations → human time moves toward exceptions, relationships, and liability → employment depends simultaneously on demand, prices, and organisational choice → net employment cannot be inferred from robot capability alone.
- **Time window**: 2027–2035.
- **Falsifier**: By 2033, across at least three sectors and three institutional settings, adoption-dense regions show neither repeatable task re-bundling nor replacement of any defined action; or comparable cross-sector whole-occupation losses appear after demand, price, and institutional explanations are excluded.
- **Leading indicator**: Robot hours, human takeover minutes, exception wages, downtime loss, training hours, and headcount recorded by task segment; annually.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-073, J-075, J-076, J-077, J-078, J-091.
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: General-purpose equipment, cheap leasing, and platform liability may integrate away whole services so quickly that a task-rebundling phase is invisible; or demand may not expand and multiple task segments may disappear together.
- **Against consensus**: **Partly consistent**: J-091 and ILO exposure material support that whole-job replacement cannot be inferred from capability, while C4’s warehouse, manufacturing, and agriculture cases supply a task-level mechanism. **Boundary**: no cross-sector series combines task frequency, takeover time, and net employment, so task re-bundling is not yet an observed social result. **Why retain**: site and liability constraints make “tasks before occupations” more testable than sudden occupational disappearance.
- **External comparison source**: EXT-41, EXT-42, EXT-43, EXT-45.
- **Source**: [C4: Embodied intelligence](../chains/40-embodied-intelligence.md#12-from-a-scene-that-pays-for-itself-to-social-consequences-bodies-change-tasks-before-they-change-relationships).

- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2027–2038, if embodied care assistance stabilises, it will first shift some family members’ hands-on work toward authorisation, coordination, payment, and exception decisions rather than removing family care relationships as a whole.

### J-101 · Embodied adoption clusters where a carrying bundle exists, widening regional differences

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-01
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2028–2038, embodied systems are more likely to form dense adoption first in regions with structured space, reliable energy, maintenance networks, insurance, and predictable liability institutions; this can create regional capability and income differences without proving global polarisation.
- **Audience scale**: The affected population is a constructed tens-of-millions impact reach of workers, firms, public services, and residents in adoption regions. Actual repeated actors are workers in dense-deployment regions using embodied equipment for a defined task weekly; only local million-scale denominators are currently observable. It raises existing professionals' ceiling; specifically, it raises existing industrial and maintenance specialists' ceiling, not equal access for regions without carriers.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 fails because cross-region actor denominators and comparable deployment densities are incomplete; Gate 2 partly passes in structured regions; Gate 3 is the core bottleneck because space, energy, maintenance, and interfaces cannot be copied; Gate 4 depends on local permits, insurers, employers, and public infrastructure; Gate 5 remains unverified because low-wage substitution gains and financing costs point in opposite directions.
- **What can be done now**: Build a regional ledger for deployment hours, repair radius, insurance, downtime, and worker transitions; do not treat a vendor demo location or shipment total as global diffusion.
- **Lens**: Regional economics, infrastructure, supply/demand, property, and institutional diffusion.
- **Reasoning chain**: Embodied systems require local carriers → carriers assemble first in a few high-wage or structured-industrial regions → deployment and maintenance networks learn → capital and talent cluster further → regional differences may widen unless public investment and institutions interrupt the feedback.
- **Time window**: 2028–2038.
- **Falsifier**: By 2035, task density, deployment hours, insurance, and retention show no systematic difference across income regions after carriers, finance, and liability institutions are controlled for; or lower-income regions lead sustained dense adoption for the same task definitions.
- **Leading indicator**: Deployment hours, repair radius, insurance, retention, downtime, energy reliability, and worker transitions by region; annually.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-073, J-075, J-076, J-077, J-078, J-090, J-093.
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Standardised leasing, remote operations, and public subsidies commoditise the carrying bundle and cross regional differences quickly; or low-wage regions attract early deployment through labour supply.
- **Against consensus**: **Partly consistent**: C4’s structured spaces, single decision-makers, and maintenance networks explain factory and large-warehouse priority, while C3 supplies an adjacent infrastructure constraint. **Boundary**: this round has no cross-region deployment, insurance, or maintenance series, so regional divergence remains an inference. **Why retain**: local carriers and liability institutions are regional inputs that models cannot copy and should precede society-scale diffusion.

### J-102 · Embodied productivity gains concentrate first in complementary assets and liability carriers

- **Proposed date**: 2026-10-01
- **One-sentence judgment**: From 2028–2038, embodied productivity gains are more likely to flow first to complementary assets and liability carriers controlling worksites, order access, finance, insurance, and maintenance, with competition, wage bargaining, and public institutions deciding whether they spread.
- **Audience scale**: The affected population is a constructed tens-of-millions impact reach of workers, firms, service users, and local governments in embodied sectors. Actual repeated actors are people performing equipment work, takeover, or liability review weekly inside adoption-dense organisations; no cross-sector deduplicated denominator exists, so the repeated-actor denominator remains million-scale at most and this remains occupational/organisational. It raises existing professionals' ceiling; specifically, it raises existing asset and liability holders' ceiling, not equal bargaining power for people without those assets.
- **Diffusion-gate review**: Gate 1 fails because distribution is not one high-frequency mass action; Gate 2 partly passes because equipment removes execution steps while adding finance, insurance, maintenance, and liability costs; Gate 3 is carried by complementary assets; Gate 4 determines authority and claims; Gate 5 remains unverified because conversion into wages, prices, hours, or public services has no series.
- **What can be done now**: In procurement and policy reviews, record equipment ownership, order and data access, accident and downtime costs, and where gains go; separate “more installed units” from “who receives gains.”
- **Lens**: Complementary assets, political economy, supply/demand, property, liability, and distribution institutions.
- **Reasoning chain**: Embodied capability earns revenue only inside sites, orders, and liability structures → organisations able to finance, insure, and absorb downtime adopt first → scarce carrying bundles gain bargaining power → gains concentrate first → competition, unions, and public institutions determine conversion into wages, lower prices, shorter hours, or public services.
- **Time window**: 2028–2038.
- **Falsifier**: By 2035, across at least three embodied sectors, income, wages, prices, and public services no longer relate to control of complementary assets, finance, and liability; or sustained, replicable gain diffusion first appears among actors without those assets.
- **Leading indicator**: Equipment leasing and ownership, accident/downtime clauses in per-unit contracts, maintenance and insurance margins, worker wages and hours, service prices, and local public spending; annually.
- **Confidence**: Medium
- **depends-on**: J-039, J-040, J-090, J-101.
- **Strongest opposing mechanism**: Platforms bundle finance, insurance, maintenance, and liability at competitive prices, rapidly eroding complementary-asset rents; or unions and public procurement lock in gain sharing from the start.
- **Against consensus**: **Partly consistent**: J-090’s complementary-asset mechanism aligns with C4’s analysis of sites, liability, and maintenance networks. **Boundary**: no cross-sector series of embodied gains, wages, prices, and public spending supports a prediction of concentration or its duration. **Why retain**: equipment performance does not contain finance, liability, or site control, so installed-unit growth cannot equal broad gains.
- **External comparison source**: EXT-45, EXT-46, EXT-47.
- **Source**: [C4: Embodied intelligence](../chains/40-embodied-intelligence.md#12-from-a-scene-that-pays-for-itself-to-social-consequences-bodies-change-tasks-before-they-change-relationships).
