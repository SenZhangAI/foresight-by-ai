# Opportunity Candidates

> Entry requires passing the gate: the scarcity cannot be automated by the same force that created the abundance, and a hard constraint must be named.

## O-001 · Ownership layer for private context

**Source:** J-SCAR-002. **Constraint:** ownership/private property and preferences that cannot be fully self-described.

The scarce asset is not memory. It is an owned, portable, authorizable, priceable record of what a person or organization tried, rejected, and why. Organizations pay to reduce rework; professionals pay to carry decision history across tools and jobs.

**Strongest counterargument:** a few interactions may reproduce preferences, reducing the asset to a short-lived cache. Watch reliable few-shot preference reconstruction.

## O-002 · Reversible infrastructure for AI action

**Source:** J-SCAR-003. **Constraint:** physical and legal irreversibility.

Sandboxes, action simulations, rollback, and traceability let an AI action run once in a structurally similar but recoverable environment. Buyers are organizations exposing AI to payment, deployment, shipping, messaging, or contracting. The price anchor is expected accident loss and insurance, not token savings.

**Strongest counterargument:** human approval may absorb the need at negligible cost. Watch procurement lists and insurance pricing for autonomous AI execution.

## O-003 · Accountable commitment layer

**Source:** J-SCAR-004. **Constraint:** legal liability belongs to an entity that can be sued and pay.

When content is abundant, “who pays if it is wrong?” becomes the usable filter. The opportunity is underwriting, evidence, and recovery around consequential AI advice in medicine, tax, compliance, engineering review, and diligence.

**Strongest counterargument:** law may assign liability to one party by fiat, removing the market layer. Watch for one-sided statutory allocation.

## Window list

General AI quality scoring and prompt/token optimization may be profitable before model providers internalize them, but they are windows rather than structural opportunities. Do not confuse temporary rent with durable scarcity.
