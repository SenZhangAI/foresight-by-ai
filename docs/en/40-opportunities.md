# Opportunity Candidates

> **Entry threshold for this file**: the scarcity must pass the [gate](00-method.md#3-the-gate-and-three-exits-not-every-scarcity-is-a-business-opportunity-and-business-opportunities-are-not-the-only-things-that-count)—it cannot be automatically eliminated by the same force that creates abundance, and it must be possible to name which hard constraint is involved.
> Anything that does not pass the gate belongs in the **Window List** at the end: it can be harvested, but it must not be treated as a structural opportunity to bet on.

## O-001 · Ownership Layer for Private Context

**Source Judgment**: J-003 ｜ **Hard Constraint**: ownership / private property + preferences that cannot be self-described

**What exactly is scarce**
It is not a “memory feature,” but **a record of trade-offs that belongs to you, can be carried with you, authorized, and priced**: who we are, what we have rejected, and why we rejected it. Today it is scattered across thousands of conversations and hundreds of killed proposals; it has never been structured, so every time we change tools we start from zero.

**Who pays, and how much**
- Organizations (primary): brand, legal, and product-line consistency translate directly into rework costs. Rework for a major redesign is usually measured in person-months; the willingness-to-pay anchor is “one fewer round of rework.”
- Professionals (secondary): they carry their style assets with them instead of leaving them in a former employer’s account.

**Why now**
After candidate solutions changed from “one at a time” to “sixty at a time,” the bottleneck moved from **capacity** to **selection** for the first time. Before this, no one needed this record because there were so few options that each could be judged manually.

**Strongest counterargument**
Preferences may be easier to reproduce than I think (see C1 Counterargument One). If a small amount of generic interaction can recover an individual’s trade-offs, this record is merely a cache that can be rebuilt in a few weeks, not an asset.
**Indicator to watch**: whether public results for few-shot preference reconstruction reach a high level of individual acceptance.

## O-002 · Reversible Infrastructure for AI Action

**Source Judgment**: J-004 ｜ **Hard Constraint**: physical + law/liability; irreversibility is a supporting lens

**What exactly is scarce**
Shadow environments, action sandboxes, one-click rollback, and step-by-step post hoc tracing—these let the act of “AI actually doing something” **run once in a place structurally equivalent to the real world, but where the consequences can be recovered**. In the world of generation, trial and error is free; in the world of execution, trial and error causes harm. There must be a layer between the two.

**Who pays, and how much**
- Any organization that lets AI touch an irreversible action: payments, deployment, shipping, sending, or signing contracts.
- The pricing anchor is not the tool fee, but **expected accident loss + insurance premiums**. This spending is benchmarked against safety and insurance budgets, not efficiency-tool budgets—which gives it a much higher ceiling.

**Why now**
AI is moving from “producing content for people to see” to “executing directly.” At that crossing, the isolation layer changes from an “optional good practice” into a prerequisite without which people will not dare to use AI.

**Strongest counterargument**
It may be absorbed on the spot by the zero-cost process of “human approval of each item,” and never grow into an independent category (see C1 Counterargument Two).
**Indicator to watch**: whether procurement lists contain separate sandbox / rollback line items; whether insurers begin pricing autonomous AI execution.

## O-003 · Accountable Commitment Layer

**Source Judgment**: J-005 ｜ **Hard Constraint**: legal liability can be borne only by a legal entity

**What exactly is scarce**
When content is unlimited and its quality is broadly adequate, content itself is no longer a filter. The only remaining usable filter is: **if this statement is wrong, who pays?** What is scarce, therefore, is not “more accurate answers,” but **a party willing to bear payment liability for an answer**, along with the underwriting, evidence-preservation, and recovery mechanisms that support such a commitment.

**Who pays, and how much**
- Scenarios where the consequences are costly and external endorsement is needed: medicine, tax, compliance, engineering review, and due diligence.
- Willingness to pay comes directly from existing professional-liability insurance and audit fees—these prices already exist and do not need to be taught to the market.

**Why now**
As long as an AI answer is merely “for reference,” responsibility remains suspended; once it begins replacing professional judgment, who bears responsibility becomes a prerequisite for whether it can be used at all. This transition is happening now.

**Strongest counterargument**
Regulators may directly prescribe the allocation of liability for AI output (for example, assigning it uniformly to the user or the provider). In that case, a market-based commitment layer would lose its reason to exist and be replaced by statutory allocation.
**Indicator to watch**: whether legislation appears that assigns liability for AI output categorically to one side.
