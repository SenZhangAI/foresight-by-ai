# Mid-term landscape: 2029–2032

> The mid-term is not a jump into science fiction. It carries the written near-term technical judgments into organizational, institutional, and real-world consequences. Every mid-term inference depends on a near-term judgment; if an upstream judgment is falsified, the downstream claim must be reviewed. Full cards live in [`90-ledger.md`](90-ledger.md).

## 1. Compute and tokens: generation stops being the bottleneck; execution boundaries become the bottleneck

If [`J-001`](90-ledger.md#j-001) and [`J-006`](90-ledger.md#j-006) hold, by around 2029 the same budget can search many candidate plans. The mid-term question is no longer whether a system can generate, but whether it can act continuously without transferring trial-and-error costs to the real world.

- **More abundant:** candidate plans, parallel reasoning, and automatic revision.
- **More scarce:** inspectable permissions, replayable state, and recoverable action.
- **Third question:** simple checks will be internalized; cross-system state, physical harm, and liability face physical and legal/liability constraints.
- **Who should change behavior:** organizations should buy observable, pausable, rollback-capable action environments before buying longer autonomous permissions.

[`J-010`](90-ledger.md#j-010) depends on [`J-009`](90-ledger.md#j-009): if constrained workflows do not stabilize first, both the time window and reliability judgment for long-horizon autonomy weaken. The strongest opposing mechanism is a vendor hiding long tasks inside many invisible short workflows and thereby bypassing explicit environment construction; confidence is medium.

## 2. Information and content: content becomes feedstock; causality becomes the product

Near-term [`J-002`](90-ledger.md#j-002) says objective selection will be internalized, while [`J-005`](90-ledger.md#j-005) moves value toward raw signals, accountable commitments, and verified causality. The uncomfortable mid-term shift is that explanations become cheap while changing reality remains expensive.

- **More abundant:** explanations, summaries, forecasts, and simulated plans for existing material.
- **More scarce:** new observations, real interventions, and entities willing to bear the cost of being wrong.
- **Third question:** synthetic material can scale automatically, but it cannot automatically complete a real experiment and a legally accepted commitment; physics, time, and liability are hard constraints.
- **Who should change behavior:** researchers and decision-makers should keep “what the model said” separate from “what reality did,” never substituting an elegant explanation for intervention evidence.

[`J-005`](90-ledger.md#j-005) has a 2029–2033 window and medium confidence, depending on [`J-001`](90-ledger.md#j-001). This may be **consistent with the common claim that data becomes more important, but external comparison is not complete**; the more specific claim here concerns unrecorded signals and compensable commitments, not data volume.

The strongest opposing mechanism is a high-fidelity world model combined with institutions that accept synthetic experiments, making real trials unnecessary. If that occurs, J-005 must be reviewed.

## 3. Attention and trust: credibility moves from expression to collateral

Near-term [`J-017`](90-ledger.md#j-017) separates weak-tie coordination from strong-tie commitment; [`J-005`](90-ledger.md#j-005) says accountable entities themselves will appreciate. If both hold, the more real content looks, the more people ask who pays when it is wrong.

- **More abundant:** personalized explanations, identity narratives, and simulated intimacy.
- **More scarce:** consistency over time, accountability, and trust willing to put assets at risk.
- **Third question:** faces, language, and instant patience can be copied; repeated exchange, relationship repair, and legal responsibility cannot be generated in one step.
- **Who should change behavior:** organizations should build liability, audit, and compensation paths around consequential AI output; individuals should treat credibility as accountability, not natural-sounding tone.

[`J-005`](90-ledger.md#j-030) is a medium-confidence structural landscape, not yet an opportunity judgment, because the institutional destination is uncertain. The strongest opposing mechanism is platforms shifting all responsibility to users while society accepts cheap, low-liability services; then a responsibility-collateral layer is a window rather than a structural opportunity.

## 4. Organizations and employment: firm boundaries are set by responsibility, not production capacity

Near-term [`J-009`](90-ledger.md#j-009) says constrained workflows mature first, and [`J-013`](90-ledger.md#j-013) says inspectable tool calls spread first. The mid-term result may not be “no people,” but a smaller number of people managing more verifiable agent processes.

- **More abundant:** decomposable, monitorable work steps and the output capacity of small teams.
- **More scarce:** organizational positions that authorize across teams, escalate exceptions, and own final responsibility.
- **Third question:** workflow coordination can be automated; responsibility, relationships, and negotiation after failure remain constrained by law and trust.
- **Who should change behavior:** hiring and education should shift from “can you perform the step?” toward “can you define boundaries, judge exceptions, and bear consequences?”

This is a medium-confidence structural landscape depending on [`J-009`](90-ledger.md#j-009) and [`J-013`](90-ledger.md#j-013). The strongest opposing mechanism is firms keeping every agent workflow inside vendors and purchasing only outcomes, hiding rather than eliminating boundary change.

## 5. Capital and power: scarcity moves from models to access

If [`J-001`](90-ledger.md#j-001)’s cost decline and [`J-005`](90-ledger.md#j-005)’s appreciation of real inputs both hold, capital will not only chase the strongest model. It will chase control of energy, data, distribution, licensing, and the ability to compensate for loss.

- **More abundant:** rentable general-purpose reasoning and generation.
- **More scarce:** low-cost energy, exclusive real-world data, critical channels, and balance sheets able to absorb accidents.
- **Third question:** software calls can be copied; ownership, energy access, relationship networks, and liability capacity do not copy at the same speed.
- **Who should change behavior:** founders should value model capability separately from access capability; regulators should watch who privatizes gains and socializes losses.

This is a medium-confidence structural landscape, not an independent opportunity card. The strongest opposing mechanism is rapid commoditization of compute, energy, and data plus timely institutional dispersal of access rights; if access prices lose structural premiums, reduce confidence.

## 6. Human needs and relationships: AI can mediate coordination, not shared experience

[`J-017`](90-ledger.md#j-017) rests on [`J-006`](90-ledger.md#j-006) and [`J-007`](90-ledger.md#j-007): communication and shared context become cheap, while strong-relationship capacity remains constrained by attention, bodies, and repeated responsibility. The most tempting mid-term mistake is treating “someone replies for you” as “someone is present for you.”

- **More abundant:** always-available companion replies, relationship-maintenance reminders, and conflict summaries.
- **More scarce:** unmediated shared experience, genuine reciprocity, and repair that the parties must personally undertake.
- **Third question:** language and reminders can be automated; embodied presence, trust, and shared consequences cannot be copied by the same generation force.
- **Who should change behavior:** families, schools, and organizations should reserve shared presence for decisions that cannot be delegated, using AI for preparation and reflection rather than substituted responsibility.

This is an Exit-B structural landscape with medium-low confidence, not a business opportunity. The strongest opposing mechanism is people treating persistent AI-mediated interaction as sufficiently reciprocal to replace shared experience; if behavior shows stable substitution, rewrite the judgment.
