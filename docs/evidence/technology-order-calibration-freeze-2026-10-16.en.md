# Technology-order calibration freeze (2026-10-16)

[中文版](technology-order-calibration-freeze-2026-10-16.md)

> This is the frozen candidate packet, not the result. It was committed before the outcome figures and post-period evaluations in the companion judgment record were collected. Every case is retrospective `CALIBRATION`: selection and later evaluation occur after the outcomes are known, so this packet cannot establish predictive accuracy or out-of-sample performance.

## 1. Candidate rule frozen before outcome collection

**R-T1 — modularity and coupling order:** At a time `T`, among candidate technical systems, the system whose repeatable unit is standardised, manufacturable in parallel, and deployable across sites with fewer site-specific interfaces, approvals, and irreversible safety liabilities should reach **both** (a) a sustained decline in comparable unit cost and (b) cross-site scale diffusion earlier. A system with strong site coupling, long approval/build cycles, or irreversible physical responsibility should be ranked later and may be judged **No** for the claim “will arrive first.”

This is a ranking and veto rule, not a claim that modular systems always win, nor a claim that invention, component cost, and system diffusion happen at the same time.

**R-T2 — stage separation:** A working prototype or a falling component price does not count as system-level success. The case must be evaluated at the first frozen window in which the same object has a comparable unit-cost measure and evidence of cross-site scale diffusion.

## 2. Frozen cases and predictions

The case set is fixed at four non-AI technologies. The comparison target is “first sustained unit-cost decline plus cross-site scale diffusion,” not “first invention.”

| ID | Case | T (information cutoff) | Prediction frozen here | Result window / test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T-CONT | Standardised container transport | 1956-04-26, Ideal X first voyage | **Yes; earliest of this set** | By 1966: comparable cargo-handling cost evidence plus multi-port / multi-mode diffusion |
| T-PV | Silicon photovoltaic generation | 1954-04-25, Bell Labs public demonstration | **Yes, but later than T-CONT** | By 2010: comparable module-price decline plus cross-site installed-capacity diffusion |
| T-NUC | Civilian nuclear power | 1954-06-27, Atomic Energy Act signed | **No for “first low-cost mass diffusion”**; later than modular contenders | By 2020: evidence on comparable cost/competitiveness and geographically broad deployment |
| T-SST | Supersonic passenger transport | 1962-11-05, US SST program authorisation period | **No for “first low-cost mass passenger diffusion”** | By 2003: evidence on repeatable fleet scale, passenger cost, route breadth, and service survival |

The labels “success” and “failure control” in the later record refer to whether the technology met the frozen target, not to a claim that this retrospective packet is a blind forecast. The two `Yes` cases are the success set; the two `No` cases are the failure controls. At least one `No` case must remain a genuine negative case rather than being rescued by changing the target after outcomes are read.

## 3. Fixed exclusion rules

- Do not substitute invention date for system diffusion date.
- Do not compare a component price with a whole-system price without marking the mismatch.
- Do not count a planning scenario, financing covenant, or retrospective narrative as a forecast of the target.
- Do not add cases, change `T`, change the target, or change the order after reading the result sources.
- If a same-object unit, denominator, or window cannot be closed, record `UNKNOWN/UNVERIFIED`; do not manufacture a hit or miss.

## 4. What would count as a rule failure

The rule fails as a candidate if either: (i) a strongly modular, low-coupling case in this frozen set reaches the target later than a strongly coupled case, contradicting the predicted order; or (ii) a frozen **No** case reaches the target first within the defined window. A counterexample must be recorded rather than explained away by changing the target.

This packet does not yet admit a lens. The judgment record must be a descendant of this commit and must report the source, locator, metric, denominator, window, and remaining gaps for each case.
