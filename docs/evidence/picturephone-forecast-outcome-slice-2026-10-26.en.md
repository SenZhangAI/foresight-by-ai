> **Status: `UNKNOWN/UNVERIFIED`.** This is a bounded reconstruction slice of a technology forecast and its outcome, not a new future forecast and not a technology-forecast accuracy sample. It expands the closest available public record of a pre-stated Picturephone target and subsequent observations field by field. Because a same-basis system total at the target date and the original forecast artifacts are not yet closed, the case cannot be coded as a definite miss.

## 1. Scope and decision rule

This slice asks one narrow question: **can AT&T's Picturephone deployment targets, published in 1969–1970, be paired with same-basis outcomes at their target dates?**

Each record retains:

- forecast source and time `T`;
- measurable target, unit, denominator, and target date;
- outcome material, outcome date, and observation window;
- status of the original-source location;
- closed fields, missing fields, and final code.

`CALIBRATION` requires the forecast object, metric, unit, denominator, and a pre-fixed observation window to match. If one load-bearing field is missing, this page stays `UNKNOWN/UNVERIFIED`; an outcome that merely looks poor cannot substitute for a pairable record.

### 2.1 Local outcome facts (not target-date outcomes)

- In the six months after the 1964 public trial, the public Picturephone rooms in New York, Chicago, and Washington recorded only 71 calls in total; ETHW also reports no calls in the final year those rooms operated. These are trial-use observations, not a 1975/1980 device denominator.
- Commercial service launched in central Pittsburgh on 1 July 1970 and expanded to central Chicago and Oak Brook in 1971. Pittsburgh reached a peak of 32 sets in 1972; Chicago reached 453 in early 1973. These are useful outcome-side facts about early diffusion resistance, but different cities, dates, and denominators cannot be combined into a target-date total.
- “A billion-dollar business” is a revenue/value metric, while the available outcome material contains device and trial-call counts. Device counts cannot stand in for revenue.

## 3. What this slice supports and does not support

### Supported boundary

1. **Original forecasts, reproduced wording, and outcome facts must be labelled separately.** What is currently locatable is ETHW's reproduction of the attribution to AT&T reports/releases; the original reports and release have not been obtained in this slice.
2. **City peaks cannot replace a system total.** The 32 and 453 figures are useful local facts about early adoption, but they do not decide a 1975 or 1980 system target.
3. **Device counts cannot replace revenue.** The same forecast sentence contains two different metrics; each requires its own outcome.
4. **The time window must align.** Results from 1972 and early 1973 cannot directly serve as results at 1975 or 1980. At most they are earlier intermediate indicators, and they do not automatically establish a final miss.

### Unsupported conclusions

- This slice does not support a fully coded `MISS` saying that AT&T's Picturephone forecast has been strictly falsified;
- it does not support “technology forecasts usually fail” or any technology-forecast accuracy rate;
- it does not generalize local Picturephone outcomes to social adoption of all video-communication technologies;
- it adds no future judgment, forecasting lens, or opportunity candidate.

## 4. How to close the case if research continues

To upgrade one record to a decidable historical pair, at least the following are needed:

1. obtain scans or stable verbatim archives of AT&T's 1969/1970 Annual Reports and the 1970 press release, and verify `T`, wording, and forecast object;
2. fix the system boundary of “sets in use” (the Bell System, the United States, or cities already in service) and whether rented devices are included;
3. obtain system-level device totals at the 1975 and 1980 target dates, matching the 50,000/25-city and 1,000,000-set targets respectively;
4. for the “billion-dollar business” target, obtain same-basis revenue, currency, and business-scope data; device counts cannot substitute;
5. record shutdown, withdrawal, or migration in the same window, so early low adoption is not packaged as a formal target-date outcome.

If the original forecast survives only as a slogan and its denominator or system boundary cannot be recovered, the final code should remain `UNKNOWN/UNVERIFIED`.
