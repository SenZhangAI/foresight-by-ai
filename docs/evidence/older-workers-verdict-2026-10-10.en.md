# Paid weekly work at 65+: verdict and check log (2026-10-10)

[中文版](older-workers-verdict-2026-10-10.md)

This file executes the [freeze record for paid weekly work at 65+](older-workers-freeze-2026-10-10.en.md). The freeze record was committed on its own before this file (`2b015b8`); the tuple, denominator definitions, exclusions, cross-country summing rule and A/B/C decision rules it fixes are only cited here, never rewritten. Every piece of evidence was published no later than the decision time T = 2026-10-10; not a single item of material published after T is used.

**Verdict: C — the D tier only (segment diffusion).** The components of the seven countries that can be counted for "aged 65 or over and working at least one hour for pay in the reference week" (the US, Japan, India, South Korea, the UK, Canada and Australia) sum to about 51.64 million before unpaid family workers are subtracted. Item 6 of the freeze record requires them to be subtracted where the survey reports this category separately; this round did not carry out that subtraction (see section 2 and gap 7 in section 4), so the strict qualifying sum is unknown and only a range can be given: the upper bound is about 51.64 million, below the S bar of 100 million, and subtracting can only lower it; the lower bound, derived from Japan and South Korea, is about 10.29 million (the South Korea item is a cross-year inference), not below 10 million. Both bounds are in the tens of millions. Mexico's about 3.5 million was seen only as relayed by the media, and the freeze has no "provisional" tier, so under the rules it counts as 0. The published employment rate of 54.5% for Japan's 65–69 group includes family workers; whether it stays above half after subtracting them was not verified, and it does not carry the verdict. This matches the expectation written down in advance at the freeze (the D tier). The judgment card registered as a result is [J-107](../en/ledger/103-110.md#j-107--paid-weekly-work-at-65-stops-at-the-d-tier-tens-of-millions-short-of-a-hundred-million), attached to section 5 of [C13 · Fiscal Squeeze](../en/chains/130-fiscal-squeeze-who-gets-cut.md#5-j-107-working-on-past-the-old-retirement-age--counted-once).

**This verdict holds only under the frozen measure.** China publishes only the age-composition shares of employed people, so no count of employed people aged 65+ in a form the freeze recognises can be obtained, and under the rules China counts as 0; yet a second-hand source puts China's employed people aged 60+ at about 99 million. As soon as one qualifying 65+ figure for China appears, the sum could cross 100 million. Everything below is written in segment terms.

**Citation conventions**: "first-hand" means a statistical agency's own data interface, data file or release page; "search-snippet only" means material whose original page could not be opened this round and of which only the search-result snippet was seen. Such material is marked separately, and the verdict does not depend on it.

## 1. Country-by-country counts

Frozen definition: in the reference week, the person worked at least one hour for pay or profit under the ILO definition of employment (self-employment included), and was aged 65 or over. The denominator is the de-duplicated number of people who performed the action in the reference week. Qualifying national components may be added, and each country contributes only one component (the latest annual average or the latest quarter). The reference period may not begin before 2023-01-01. A country that publishes only 60+ and cannot isolate 65+ counts as 0. Unpaid contributing family workers: subtract them where the survey reports them separately. Where it does not, mark the component "broad definition". It may then enter the scale description for D, but if reaching S depends on that component, S may not be awarded (item 6 of the freeze record, verbatim; the original's bold is omitted here). How this was carried out in this round is shown in the "Unpaid family workers" column of the table below and in gap 7 of section 4: the subtraction was not carried out.

**Sum before unpaid family workers are subtracted** (in millions): 11.374 + 9.43 + 23.15 + 4.147 + 1.654 + 1.130 + 0.758 ≈ **51.64 million** (seven countries); without India, about 28.49 million. This is the **upper bound** of the strict qualifying sum; subtracting can only lower it. **Lower bound**: Japan's 9.43 million minus the 2.39 million "self-employed workers and family workers" combined, at least 7.04 million; South Korea's 4.147 million minus the 899,200 unpaid family workers of all ages in 2023, about 3.25 million (cross-year, an inference; the separate count for 2025 was not verified); the two together, about 10.29 million. The strict qualifying sum lies between about 10.29 million and 51.64 million, and both ends are between 10 million and 100 million. Mexico counts as 0; if about 3.5 million is later verified from the statistical agency's original table, the upper bound becomes about 55.14 million, still below 100 million.

**Sub-population majority**: the freeze recognises only the **standard age groups publicly released** by the same qualifying survey. Japan publishes a 65–69 group, with an employment rate of 54.5%, above half on the published figure; but this published figure includes family workers, the freeze requires them to be subtracted, and this round did not obtain the separate count for the 65–69 group, so whether the group stays above half after subtraction is **not verified**, and this route does not carry the verdict. South Korea publishes only open-ended groups such as 65 and over, 70 and over and 75 and over; the 65–69 employment rate of about 54% derived by subtraction is not a published figure and is **not counted**. The United States publishes a 65–69 row, but this round did not read it; Canada publishes the number employed aged 65–69, but its employment rate was not read; for the United Kingdom and Australia the highest group is 65 and over (12.7% and 15.6%), not above half.

## 2. Gate-by-gate verdict

| Gate | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Gate 1 · Scale | **Fail**; judged **C · D tier only** | The strict qualifying sum is unknown (the subtraction of unpaid family workers was not carried out); the range is about 10.29 million to 51.64 million: the upper bound is below the S bar of 100 million, so A is excluded; the lower bound is ≥ 10 million, so the sum route to the D tier holds (the South Korea item in the lower bound is a cross-year inference; it depends neither on Mexico nor on India); the route of above half in Japan's 65–69 group is unverified and does not carry the verdict |
| Gates 2 to 5 | **Not evaluated** | Frozen rule: when the sum of qualifying components does not reach 100 million, they are not evaluated, and may not be written as "passed" |
| L8 demand-side review | **Not evaluated** | As above |

Three details of the verdict that need to be stated:

- **S was not awarded.** The upper bound is about 51.64 million, and any subtraction can only lower it, so the exclusion of A is not affected by the gap in the subtraction; without the India component, the upper bound is about 28.49 million.
- **The execution gap on item 6 of the freeze record**: the freeze says to subtract them "where the survey reports them separately. Where it does not, mark the component 'broad definition'". The surveys of the US, Japan and South Korea are confirmed to report unpaid family workers separately, so the subtraction is required; for India, the UK, Canada and Australia this round did not verify whether they are reported separately, and they are treated strictly as requiring subtraction. This round obtained no country's separate 65+ count, so the subtraction was not carried out, and the about 51.64 million in section 1 is not the qualifying sum, only its upper bound. The C verdict is carried instead by the lower bound: Japan at least 7.04 million (9.43 million minus the 2.39 million "self-employed workers and family workers" combined), South Korea about 3.25 million (4.147 million minus the 899,200 unpaid family workers of all ages in 2023; using the 2023 all-age count as an upper bound for the 2025 65+ group is a cross-year inference, not verified), about 10.29 million together, only about 0.29 million above 10 million; the non-helper part of the US component could only raise the lower bound, but this round has no separate count for it, so it is not counted. Because of the same gap, the route of above half in Japan's 65–69 group is recorded as unverified.
- **Compared with the expectation at the freeze**: the freeze record expected the D tier in advance, and the result is C (D tier). Agreement between expectation and result is not evidence; screening power has to be tested by rules that can say no — see the next section.

## 3. Why no separate control group

Section 3 of the freeze record states: "It lists no separate controls that must be vetoed. The decision rules above are already open to two negative outcomes, B and C. The sieve's screening power is tested in the other freeze record from the same day, cited above." This file follows it and adds no control group after the fact. The [verdict and check log for social actions that do not presuppose AI](non-ai-social-action-verdict-2026-10-10.en.md), from the same day, tested working from home and five controls that must be vetoed against the same set of thresholds, and all five controls were vetoed at Gate 1. The action in this file stops at C under the same thresholds, which shows that the thresholds did not let every candidate through.

## 4. Gaps

1. **The EU**: Eurostat's data interface (`lfsa_egan`, aged 65 and over) was refused from this environment, so no figure from 2023 onward was read, and under the rules the EU counts as 0. A search snippet of an annual European Commission report gives a 2023 employment rate of 15.2% for ages 65–69; it is a share only, with no head count, and is not counted.
2. **China**: only a second-hand figure in share form was obtained, and only for 60 and over. The freeze recognises "a share multiplied by the same office's contemporaneous 65+ population base", but the Chinese yearbook publishes the age-composition shares of employed people, and what they are multiplied by is total employment, which is not that form. This is the largest coverage gap in this verdict; see falsifier (4) of [J-107](../en/ledger/103-110.md#j-107--paid-weekly-work-at-65-stops-at-the-d-tier-tens-of-millions-short-of-a-hundred-million).
3. **Indonesia**: the statistics agency's pages returned 403; obtaining the data failed.
4. **Brazil**: there is only a 60-and-over group; a 65+ figure cut from the microdata by ourselves would not be an agency-published figure, and is not counted.
5. **South Korea, 65–69**: the about 54% derived by subtraction is not a published figure and is not counted toward the sub-population route.
6. **Mexico**: there is only a media report, and the original table was not read; the freeze has no "provisional" tier, so under the rules it counts as 0. If about 3.5 million is verified from the statistical agency's original table, the upper bound becomes about 55.14 million, and the verdict is unchanged.
7. **The subtraction of unpaid family workers was not carried out (an execution gap)**: the components of all seven countries include unpaid family workers who meet each country's hour threshold (15 hours in the US, 18 hours in South Korea, 1 hour elsewhere). Item 6 of the freeze record requires them to be subtracted where the survey reports them separately; the US, Japan and South Korea are confirmed to report them separately, and the other four countries were not verified and are treated strictly. This round obtained no country's separate 65+ count, so the subtraction was not carried out. Consequences: the strict qualifying sum is unknown, and only a range of about 10.29 million to 51.64 million can be given; the exclusion of A is not affected; the C sum route depends on the lower bound, of which the South Korea item is an inference; the route of above half in Japan's 65–69 group is recorded as unverified. The remedy is to obtain each country's cross-tabulation of people aged 65+ by status in employment and recompute under the same frozen measure, without rewriting the freeze record.
8. **India's population base**: the population in PLFS Table 6 is a sampling-design estimate, and the report notes that it is not to be used to provide current population estimates, so the head count should be read as an order of magnitude.
9. **The US 2025 annual average** is an 11-month average; the 2024 annual average was 11.276 million, of the same magnitude.

## 5. What this page does not say

- It does not say "older people working is already a universal phenomenon", nor that it will become a social norm: under the frozen measure it reaches only the tens of millions.
- It does not say that employment in old age in China or the EU is small: they count as 0 because no qualifying figure could be obtained, not because they were measured as 0.
- It does not say that this scale is caused by the fiscal squeeze: this page counts only the action already happening at T; the mechanism is explained in [C13](../en/chains/130-fiscal-squeeze-who-gets-cut.md) and in J-107's forward claim, each with its own falsifiers.
- It does not evaluate Gates 2 to 5 or L8, and it does not write "not evaluated" as passed.
- It does not rewrite the freeze record; if a definition later proves unworkable, the only allowed move is to register "frozen definition failed" in a new file.
