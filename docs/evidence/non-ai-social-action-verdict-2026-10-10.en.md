# Verdict and check log for social actions that do not presuppose AI (2026-10-10)

[中文版](non-ai-social-action-verdict-2026-10-10.md)

This file executes the [freeze record for countable social actions that do not presuppose AI](non-ai-social-action-freeze-2026-10-10.en.md). The freeze record was committed on its own before this file (`647801a`); the tuples, denominator definitions, exclusions, cross-country summing rule and A/B/C decision rules it fixes are only cited here, never rewritten. Every piece of evidence was published no later than the decision time T = 2026-10-10; not a single item of material published after T is used.

**Verdict: C — the main candidate reaches only tier D (segment diffusion).** The qualifying national components that can be counted for "doing at least part of one's paid work at one's own home in the reference week" sum to about 48 million, all of them "broad definition"; even adding the EU, whose base was not obtained, gives only about 66 million, below the S bar of 100 million. It is the practice of more than half of people at work in several standard occupation and industry breakdowns published by the US official survey. The five controls that must be vetoed (M1–M5) were all vetoed at Gate 1, as expected at the freeze. The judgment card registered as a result is [J-104](../en/ledger/103-110.md#j-104--paid-work-from-home-stops-at-the-d-tier-tens-of-millions-weekly-a-plateau-since-2023-and-the-employer-decides). This verdict is not attached to any reasoning-chain text.

**This is a segment-level conclusion, not a social-scale conclusion.** Everything below is written in segment terms; it is not written as a trend covering the entire population.

**Citation conventions**: "first-hand" means a statistical agency's or company's own page, data file or regulatory filing; "opened via archive" means a first-hand page opened this round from an Internet Archive capture (the original site is not directly reachable from this environment); "search-snippet only" means material whose original page could not be opened this round and of which only the search-result snippet was seen. Such material is not the basis of any verdict; it is listed only so that it can be re-checked.

## 1. Evidence for the main candidate

Frozen definition: in a reference week, a person does at least part of their **paid work** at their own home, where that job's regular workplace is not the home. The denominator is the de-duplicated number of people who performed the action in the reference week; qualifying national components may be added, each country contributes only one component, and where a weekly measure exists it is the one used; a country without a qualifying survey counts as 0; the reference period may not begin before 2023-01-01; the S bar is a sum ≥ 100 million.

### 1.2 Sum and verdict

- **Counted in the sum**: United States 34.68 million + United Kingdom 12.76 million + New Zealand 0.90 million ≈ **48.34 million**. All three components are "broad definition".
- **What adding the EU would do**: estimating at the EU's employment order of magnitude of about 200 million (this round obtained no citable source for that base), the EU would contribute about 18 million, for a total of about 66 million.
- **Upper-bound argument**: for the sum to reach 100 million, the EU component would have to be at least 51.66 million; at a share of 8.9%, the employment base would need to be at least about 580 million, more than the EU's entire population (Eurostat puts the EU population on 2025-01-01 at about 450.4 million, search-snippet only, <https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20250711-1>). So even setting the base aside, S cannot be reached under the frozen rules.
- **The part that is not broad**: only the US wage-and-salary measure, 28.43 million. The frozen rules say that if an S verdict depends on a broad-definition component, S may not be granted; this clause was not needed this round, because even the broad sum falls short of 100 million.
- **Not B**: the United States, the United Kingdom and New Zealand all have qualifying weekly components, and Gate 2 can name the frozen old action; none of B's three conditions holds.
- **Both routes to tier D hold at once**: the sum reaches the tens-of-millions scale; and the action is done by more than half of people inside standard breakdowns published by a qualifying survey (see 1.3).

**Verdict: C · tier D (segment diffusion).**

In the same table, no age group is above half; for people aged 25 and over with a bachelor's degree or higher the share is 37.8% (Table A-41). Occupations that require physical presence have very low telework rates: service 6.0%, food preparation 0.9%, construction and extraction 2.5%, production 5.8%, transportation and material moving 2.9%, healthcare practitioners 12.0%, healthcare support 12.8%. The three groups of service; construction, maintenance and natural resources; and production and transportation together make up about 38.7% of people at work (59.392 million / 153.406 million); adding healthcare practitioners brings it to about 45%.

**Which kind of action this is**: it mainly neither "raises existing professionals' ceiling" nor "lets people who could not do it do it now"; it changes where people already employed do their work — what the freeze record defines is a work location, not a new capability. Only a small part may belong to the latter — in Table A-41 the telework rate of people at work with a disability is 24.4%, above the overall 21.9% — but how many of them can hold a job only because of this was not measured this round.

## 2. Gate-by-gate verdict on the main candidate

| Gate | Verdict | Basis (all no later than T) | What is missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gate 1 · Scale | **Fail** (tens-of-millions scale) | See section 1: the counted sum is about 48.34 million, about 66 million with the EU added, all broad definition; the only figure that is not broad is the US wage-and-salary measure, 28.43 million | Qualifying coverage is only three countries; the EU employment base was not obtained |
| Gate 2 · Replacement | **Pass (counted per occasion; replacement in total volume is supported by an official series)** | The frozen old action is "one round trip to the regular workplace for the same job". Counted per occasion, a full day at home necessarily removes one commute round trip. Total volume has series too: commuting trips in England's National Travel Survey (NTS, which covers England only) were 111 per person per year in 2024 and 112 in 2025, about 20% fewer than in 2019, and the government text says this "may suggest a shift towards working from home and hybrid working" (<https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/national-travel-survey-2025/nts-2025-trips-by-purpose-age-mode-and-sex>, released 2026-09-10, first-hand); in the American Community Survey (ACS), the share of people who usually work from home was 5.7% in 2019, 13.8% in 2023 and 13.3% in 2024 (Census page, opened via archive). This is a stronger Gate 2 than in the previous round's J-103: there, only per-occasion replacement held, and replacement in total volume was disputed | About half of teleworkers are at home only for "some of their hours", which does not necessarily remove a whole commute; transport researchers offer the opposing view that "commutes are replaced by other trips, and vehicle-kilometres do not necessarily fall" (Fehr & Peers, search-snippet only) |
| Gate 3 · Carrier | **Pass** | No dedicated facility has to be built for this: home broadband, personal computers and phones already exist for other reasons; Pew said in 2025 that 78% of US adults have home broadband (<https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/internet-broadband/>, first-hand) | Home-broadband and living-space constraints outside the United States were not obtained |
| Gate 4 · Decision | **Partial pass: the decision lies with the employer, not the person** | The person cannot decide alone and needs the employer's consent; inside each employer this is a local closed loop (Gate 4's clause (c)), so it does not block the action itself, but it decides where the action stops. At T there was already empirical evidence: after a US presidential memorandum of 2025-01-20 directed agencies to "terminate remote work arrangements" (<https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/memorandum-return-person-work>, first-hand), Gallup said the share of federal employees working hybrid fell from 61% to 28%; Amazon has required five days a week in the office since 2025-01-02 (CNBC 2024-09-18); Microsoft, three days a week by the end of February 2026 (<https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/09/09/flexible-work-update/>, first-hand); UK civil servants, at least 60% in the office (gov.uk, first-hand); UK law gives only a right to "request flexible working", which the employer can refuse on legitimate business grounds (<https://www.gov.uk/flexible-working>, first-hand); among hybrid workers only 34% decide for themselves which days they work at home (Gallup, <https://www.gallup.com/workplace/713093/hybrid-work-productivity.aspx>) | A systematic count of employers' office-attendance requirements across countries; countries where the law grants the right to the worker (if any) were not obtained |
| Gate 5 · Recurring net burden | **Pass** | Relative to the incumbent (commuting to the workplace), each occasion saves commuting time and cost; what is added is the burden of coordination and isolation at home. Voluntary repeat use had already happened at T: in the Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes (SWAA) run by Stanford and other institutions, the share of paid working days worked entirely at home was 7.15% in 2019 and 26%–29% in 2023–2026 (<https://wfhresearch.com/datafiles/WFHtimeseries_monthly.json>, 2026-09 vintage, first-hand); on average workers value working from home as equivalent to a pay rise of about 8% (Bloom, as relayed by CNBC 2025-02-07) | Net burden is not directly measured; SWAA is an online sample |

**L8 demand-side review: no veto, but it places the equilibrium at hybrid working.** It does not require human nature to change: the demand to save time and commute less has long existed. Two of L8's anchors, "the sense of presence" and "real relationships", do work here: Gallup says that among remote-capable jobs 52% are hybrid, 26% fully remote and 22% fully on-site, and that this structure has "held relatively stable since mid-2022". What most people choose is to be at home part of the time, not to stay away from the workplace altogether.

**Necessary non-technical premises (two; delete either one and the conclusion fails):**

1. **Occupational structure.** About 39% of people at work are in service, construction and maintenance, production and transportation occupations, whose telework rates are only 0.9%–12.8%; adding healthcare practitioners brings it to about 45%. This is why the scale stops at the tens of millions and why the action is above half only in a few white-collar breakdowns. Delete it — suppose most jobs could be done at home — and the conclusion "stops at tier D" loses its grounds.
2. **The employer's decision right (Gate 4).** Whether to work at home is decided by the employer, and employers had already tightened on a large scale in 2025. Delete it — suppose the person could decide alone — and, given SWAA's valuation (about an 8% pay rise), the share working at home would keep rising, and the "plateau" conclusion would not hold.

Both are premises of supply and demand, organisation and institutions, not technical premises. **AI is not part of this mechanism**: delete AI and the conclusion holds unchanged.

**Conclusion**: under the frozen rules, the main candidate is **C · tier D (segment diffusion)**. None of Gates 2 to 5 vetoes; Gate 1 stops it at the tens-of-millions scale.

## 4. Gap list

1. **Asymmetric evidence coverage (this round's most important finding; reported only, no rule added).** M2 and the main candidate were vetoed at Gate 1 partly because "a country without a qualifying survey counts as 0", not because their scale was shown to be below the bar: in the EU in 2019, weekly carers alone numbered about 70 million; China, India and Japan have no qualifying figures for either working from home or care. Single-platform digital actions with globally self-reported user counts (such as J-103) clear Gate 1 more easily, while offline social actions that depend on national statistics are capped by survey coverage. This is a measurement asymmetry of this sieve. This round registers it and does not change the frozen rules or any gate on its basis; addressing it would require a separate calibration.
2. **The weak point of the "usually" measure.** The freeze record says that "usually working from home (at home on at least half of working days in the reference period)" necessarily means at least weekly. Eurostat's "usually" uses a four-week reference period and is computed on more than half of working time, which does not strictly guarantee at least once in every week. The EU did not enter the sum this round, so the conclusion is unaffected; the freeze file is not changed, and if this measure is relied on later, "frozen definition failed" must first be registered separately.
3. **BLS's denominator is "at work", not all employed**: people on vacation or sick leave are not in the denominator. Whether this pushes the share up or down was not assessed.
4. **The UK component multiplies a Great Britain share by a UK-wide base**: Northern Ireland is not in the share's sample, so the head count may be slightly overstated.
5. **Table A-42 was obtained only up to July 2026** (the latest issue that could be opened in the archive), so the breakdown shares are not September's.
6. **Gate 4's constraint is not independent of Gate 1.** See the last paragraph of section 3.
7. **The EU employment base was not obtained**, and the EU population was seen only in a search snippet; both are used only in the upper-bound argument, and the verdict does not depend on them.
8. **The US figure for M2 matches labels by position in the table**; the column labels of the archived page were not checked one by one; without it, M2's qualifying sum is lower and the veto is unchanged.
9. **The opposing view on Gate 2** (commutes replaced by other trips) was seen only in search snippets; no counter-series on total trips or vehicle-kilometres was found.

## 5. What this verdict does not say

- It does not say that working from home has reached social scale. The qualifying components sum to the tens-of-millions scale; it is above half only within a few white-collar occupation and industry breakdowns.
- It does not say that fewer than 100 million people worldwide work from home. Under the frozen rules a country without a qualifying survey counts as 0, and the true global count may be higher; this verdict says only that under the frozen measure **the evidence does not support** tier S.
- It does not say that working from home is receding. BLS's telework rate has stayed between 21.5% and 23.8% since 2024, averaging about 22.1% in January–September 2026 against about 22.4% in the same period a year earlier; this is a plateau, not an ebb. The forward claim is written in J-104, with its own falsifiers.
- It does not say that Gates 2 to 5 have been shown to have independent veto power.
